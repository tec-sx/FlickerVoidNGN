# Vehicles

- Vehicles (the `Vehicles` module over Runtime, milestone 1 of the creation tools; `game.AddVehicles()`) are made in the editor's vehicle creator, opened by a double-click on a placed vehicle (in place) or on a vehicle file in the asset browser (alone); the asset browser shows them with their own icon and filter, and New, Vehicle writes a car on four wheels. A vehicle is a creation: it owns the files beside it named after it (`Van.blend`, `Van.png`), which the browser groups under its tile and renames, moves and duplicates with it, and the builder leaves Blender files out. Its panel (`VehiclePanelViewModel`, Editor Assets) shows the vehicle's type, its wheel count among those the type takes and its parts, and writes each choice through a `VehicleDocument`: held unsaved, one undo step each, and seen by every load of the file, so placed copies follow it as they follow a change on disk. A `.fvvehicle` (`VehicleFile`, JSON with the `vehicle` schema) holds the `type` (`car` or `motorcycle`), the `body` and the `wheels`, each a `VehiclePart`: its `place` (its pivot, in the vehicle's space) and its `mesh`, a closed `SolidMesh`. Vehicles face +Z with left at +X. Wheels are listed front axle first, left before right, and part ids follow from the order (`GetParts`): `body`, then `wheel.1L` and `wheel.1R`, or `wheel.1` where an axle has one wheel (a motorcycle on two). A car takes 4, 6 or 8 wheels and a motorcycle 2 or 4 (`GetWheelCounts`) until driving (milestone 4) settles the full rules. A file with another count, or with a mesh that isn't closed, can't be read, so the content check reports it. `Create` makes a box body over two axles of cylinder wheels sized for the type. `SetWheelCount` adds axles behind the last or removes the last ones and leaves every other wheel as it was; the body's rear follows the last axle (owner, 2026-11-25): every point of its mesh behind the last axle moves along the vehicle as far as the last axle did, so the body reaches as far behind the wheels as before, longer or shorter, and its front stays. `SetType` takes the count nearest the one the vehicle has that the new type takes, and keeps the wheels while they sit the same way, one or two to an axle, the body's rear following the last axle the same way; wheels made new leave the body as it is.
- A vehicle is placed in a level as an entity with the `vehicle` component (`Vehicle`: the `file`, a `.fvvehicle`). `AddVehicles` (once per game, however often it is called) adds `Creations` (`game.AddCreations()`) and gives it the vehicle kind (`VehicleSpawnSystem`), so its parts spawn nested under the entity in the editor and the game, each drawn with a `MeshRenderer` named `<file>#<part id>` (`Vehicles/Van.fvvehicle#wheel.1L`) that `VehicleMeshSource`, added to the game's `MeshSources`, makes from the file's mesh. Files are read through the game's `IContentManager` service (`SceneHost` and the editor register theirs): without it, as in the content check, a vehicle spawns no parts. When the file changes on disk or the editor writes it, its vehicles are patched by part id (`CreationParts.Patch`), so kept parts stay the same entities, and the meshes are made again. A file that can't be read keeps the parts and meshes of its last good version and logs a warning (`VehicleFiles`); one that never could spawns none. The editor's game adds vehicles before the project's modules, and the template's launcher adds them to its game. In the editor a vehicle file dragged into the viewport places a vehicle named after it, and the vehicle is handled like any entity: clicking a part selects it, the selection box and F take in its parts, its parts follow the gizmo and are hidden with it, a duplicate spawns parts of its own, and deleting it takes its parts (undo spawns them again).
- Doors (milestone 2): a vehicle file has a `doors` list of parts, ids `door.1`, `door.2` in order, after the wheels in `GetParts`; changing the wheels or the type leaves them alone. A part may have a `joint` (a hinge or slider, `PartJoint` in `Creations.md`) and a `toggle` (the offer action that opens and shuts it, such as `Interaction.Action.Toggle`); a part spawned with them gets a `PartJoint` and a `Toggle`, so a door swings when its toggle changes. The door tool writes these; until it exists a door is added by editing the file.

A part's `mirror` switch (`VehiclePart.Mirror`, written only when on) tells the creator to edit its mesh the same on both sides of its centre line, the part's X = 0 plane; the engine only keeps it.

Collision from the greybox (vehicles reference Physics): each part spawned from a vehicle file gets a body from its own mesh. A standing part (no joint) is a `StaticBody` whose `TriangleCollider` is the mesh's triangles, so its walls and openings are exact; a part with a joint (a door) is a kinematic `RigidBody` with a `HullCollider` of its points, which paths go around and which follows its transform as the joint swings it, so a closed door blocks a character until it opens. The bodies exist where the game has physics (`AddPhysics`), in play only for the greyboxes' sake: the components are inert without it. A hull is convex, so a door with a window is solid in the physics; a closed-body door is only as exact as its hull.
