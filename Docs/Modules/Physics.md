# Physics

- One physics engine for the whole game (owner, 2026-11-25): Jolt, through JoltPhysicsSharp, chosen over Bepu, Box3D and PhysX for its finished vehicles and character controller (the spike's report is linked from issue #151). It replaced BepuPhysics in task 1.0 of the editor creation tools' milestone 1; hull and triangle colliders, hinges and sliders follow.
- The library is this module's detail: `IPhysicsSystem` (a fixed system and service, added by `game.AddPhysics()`) and the components speak engine types only, so other modules and tests never reach Jolt and a later move touches this module alone. It casts rays (`RayCast`, the nearest body's entity, point, normal and distance, optionally ignoring one entity's body; a ray that starts inside a body hits it at once) and answers `HasBody`, `IsAwake` and `BodyCount`.
- Scene components: `rigidBody` (`RigidBody`: a collider, a mass, kinematic or dynamic, and whether paths go around it) and `staticBody` (`StaticBody`: a collider, an obstacle by default). Colliders are `box`, `sphere` and `capsule`, chosen by `type`, with an `offset` from the entity. Every body has friction 1, no bounce and damping of 5% a second, under a gravity of 9.8.
- Each step adds bodies for new components, removes those whose entity or component is gone, moves kinematic bodies to their transform over the step (so they push what they meet, and stop when it stands still) and static bodies to a transform that moved (waking what rested on them), steps, and copies the poses of awake dynamic bodies to their transforms. Entities carry an internal `SimulatedBody` with the pose they were last placed at, so bodies that stand still cost no call into Jolt. A body at rest falls asleep. Solid greyboxes without a body of their own get a static box of their size while playing.
- Jolt is native code: `joltc` (about 2 MB) sits beside a NativeAOT game, from the JoltPhysics.Native package, for Windows, Linux, macOS and Android. Jolt starts once per process; each `PhysicsSystem` has its own worker threads, one fewer than the cores. A level that adds more than 64 bodies in one step has its broad phase rebuilt for fast queries.
