# Physics

- One physics engine for the whole game (owner, 2026-11-25): Jolt, through JoltPhysicsSharp, chosen over Bepu, Box3D and PhysX for its finished vehicles and character controller (the spike's report is linked from issue #151). It replaced BepuPhysics in task 1.0 of the editor creation tools' milestone 1; hull and triangle colliders follow. Hinges and sliders are kinematic, not physics constraints (owner, 2026-11-25): a creation's jointed parts are posed by the `JointSystem` (`Creations.md`), and doors stay kinematic.
- The library is this module's detail: `IPhysicsSystem` (a fixed system and service, added by `game.AddPhysics()`) and the components speak engine types only, so other modules and tests never reach Jolt and a later move touches this module alone. It casts rays (`RayCast`, the nearest body's entity, point, normal and distance, optionally ignoring one entity's body; a ray that starts inside a body hits it at once) and answers `HasBody`, `IsAwake` and `BodyCount`.
- Scene components: `rigidBody` (`RigidBody`: a collider, a mass, kinematic or dynamic, and whether paths go around it) and `staticBody` (`StaticBody`: a collider, an obstacle by default). Colliders are `box`, `sphere`, `capsule`, `hull` and `triangles`, chosen by `type`, with an `offset` from the entity. A hull (`HullCollider`) is the smallest convex shape holding its `points`, in the entity's space, the collision of a moving part; points on a line or a plane are refused with the entity's name, as Jolt would stop the process on them. Triangles (`TriangleCollider`: `positions` and `indices`, three per triangle, wound as the engine's models, clockwise seen from the front, and turned round for Jolt) collide from their front and are the exact collision of standing things, so only static bodies take them; a rigid body with triangles is refused, pointing to a hull. Every body has friction 1, no bounce and damping of 5% a second, under a gravity of 9.8.
- Each step adds bodies for new components, removes those whose entity or component is gone, moves kinematic bodies to their transform over the step (so they push what they meet, and stop when it stands still) and static bodies to a transform that moved (waking what rested on them), steps, and copies the poses of awake dynamic bodies to their transforms. Entities carry an internal `SimulatedBody` with the pose they were last placed at, so bodies that stand still cost no call into Jolt. A body at rest falls asleep. Solid greyboxes without a body of their own get a static box of their size while playing, or, when they were shaped (`Greybox.Mesh`), a static body of the shape's own triangles, which paths walk as they are.
- Jolt is native code: `joltc` (about 2 MB) sits beside a NativeAOT game, from the JoltPhysics.Native package, for Windows, Linux, macOS and Android. Jolt starts once per process; each `PhysicsSystem` has its own worker threads, one fewer than the cores, but its C API steps every simulation with the one temporary allocator it starts with, so simulations on different threads (games tested side by side) step one at a time. A level that adds more than 64 bodies in one step has its broad phase rebuilt for fast queries.
- Wheeled bodies (milestone 4 of the creation tools): a `wheeledBody` (`WheeledBody`: the chassis `collider`, `mass`, the `wheels`, engine and brake torque, suspension travel, frequency and damping, `weightDrop`, `leans`) is a dynamic body on Jolt's vehicle constraint, facing +Z with its left at +X. Each `BodyWheel` (its centre at rest in the entity's space, radius, width, `maxSteer` in degrees, `driven`, `handBrake`) is not a body: it hangs on its suspension and casts a cylinder onto the ground. Driven wheels that follow each other on one axle share a differential, a driven wheel alone has its own, and the engine's torque is split evenly among them. A `DriveInput` beside it (forward -1 to 1, right -1 to 1, brake, hand brake; set by game code each step, never saved) drives it, and waking input wakes a sleeping body. `leans` uses Jolt's motorcycle controller, which leans the body into turns and holds it up. `IPhysicsSystem.TryGetWheelPose` gives a wheel's place in the entity's space (on its suspension, steered and rolled), `IsWheelOnGround` whether it touches, and `GetVelocity` a moving body's velocity. Taking the component off takes the body and its wheels out of the simulation.
