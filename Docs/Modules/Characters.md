# Characters, paths and senses

- Characters are point and click (owner, 2026-11-21: point and click with the orthographic view; no third-person camera). The `Characters` module (over Physics) has `CharacterMover` (scene component `characterMover`: speed, turn speed, arrive distance) and `CharacterMovementSystem` (`game.AddCharacters()`, after `AddPhysics` when the game has physics): a `MoveCommand` event sends a character walking to a point (a `Destination` component while it walks), it walks straight there turning to face its way (characters face +Z, the front of a glTF model) and standing on the ground a physics ray finds (within `StepHeight` of its feet; ground further below, it falls onto at `FallSpeed`, walking or standing still; a character nested under another entity is carried by it and stays off the ground), and sends `ArrivedEvent`; `StopCommand` stops it. A `SteerCommand` steers it along a direction instead (a `Heading` component while it walks; speed scales with the direction's length, at most 1, and a zero direction stops it); steering, walking to a point and turning in place replace each other, and a steered step stays on the ground the path finder knows (`IPathFinder.Constrain`, Detour's move along the surface), so walls stop it. Paths around obstacles come from the `Ai` module (over Characters, on DotRecast; `game.AddAi(settings)` after `AddCharacters`): `PathSystem` (a service and the movement system's `IPathFinder`) builds a navigation mesh (`NavigationMesh`, one Recast tile, agent radius, height, climb and slope from `NavigationSettings`) from the boxes around every static body (`StaticGeometry`), again when static bodies come or go, and a `MoveCommand` then walks its corners one by one; a point off the mesh is reached as near as the mesh allows, and a move with no way there stops the character. `PathSystem.TryFindRandomPoint` picks a point of the ground within a radius (Detour, with a given `Random`). `FaceCommand` turns a character in place (a `Facing` component while it turns). Perception (`Ai/Senses`, ported from the Unreal AI controller's sight and hearing; no damage sense, as there is little combat): a `perception` component (sight radius, lose-sight radius, sight angle either side of +Z, eye height, hearing range, memory) sees `perceivable` entities, and `PerceptionSystem` (a fixed system and service, `GetSeen`, `GetHeard`) sends `TargetSeenEvent` and `TargetLostEvent` as they come into view within the sight radius with no body in between (`IPhysicsSystem.RayCast` can ignore the perceiver's own body) and leave it past the lose-sight radius, and hears a `NoiseEvent` within its hearing range times the loudness and the noise's range (`NoiseHeardEvent`), remembering it for its memory (`NoiseForgottenEvent`). A click becomes a world ray with `CameraView.GetRay` (Rendering, over `Ray.FromViewProjection` in Runtime's `Geometry`, which the editor's picking shares) and meets the world through `IPhysicsSystem.RayCast`, which returns the nearest body's entity, point and normal.
- Bodies follow their transforms: a `rigidBody` is dynamic (moved by physics, with a mass) or `kinematic` (following its entity's transform as code moves it, such as a door or a character; it pushes dynamic bodies and is never pushed), and a `staticBody` that code moves follows too. Paths go around obstacles (static bodies unless they turn `obstacle` off, as pickups do; rigid bodies that turn it on, such as doors): `ObstacleGeometry` gives the boxes around them as triangles, and a triangle collider's own triangles, so the floors, walls and openings of a standing creation are walked as they are, the navigation mesh is built in tiles (`NavigationSettings.TileSize`), and an obstacle that moves or comes to rest builds only the tiles it touches again.
- Perceivers look once every `sightInterval` (0.1 s by default, 0 for every fixed step), taking turns, so their sight rays spread over the steps.
