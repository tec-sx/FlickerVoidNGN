# The editor

- The look (owner, 2026-11-26, `Docs/Design/EditorUi.md`): palette A, Ember, in `Shell/Themes/EditorTheme.axaml` (warm browns, one amber accent), panels meeting along 1 px lines; `EditorThemeTests` check contrast, warmth and that views use no colour the theme does not declare. The theme is the kit every view uses, and a view is built from its parts, not from Fluent's defaults: 22 px rows, `Switch` toggles for booleans, number fields dragged sideways with X, Y and Z letters in the axes' colours and a fill for ranged ones (`DragNumberBox`), panel cards for every section of a panel (`Controls/PanelCard`, below), `Border.searchRow` search rows, `Border.rail` category rails, `Border.viewportBar` pills, `Border.kbd` keys, and Lucide icons (`LucideIcons`, written by the design's `icons.py` from `lucide-static`; a new icon is added there). The text is Inter (`WithInterFont`). The window draws no title: the theme replaces Fluent's `WindowDrawnDecorations` (which writes the title over the client area) with caption buttons only.
- The window (`MainWindow.axaml`) is a 30 px top bar, the working area and a 24 px strip. The top bar: the project menu (the project's name: Open project, Save, Save assets, Discard asset edits, Materials, Project settings, Bake light probes, Open in Visual Studio), the level's tab and one tab per open document (below), the command box in the middle, and at the right the unsaved chip (`UnsavedCount`, `SaveEverythingCommand`), undo and redo, Compile, Play and the gear (`MainWindowViewModel.TopBar.cs`). The strip: the Content and Day tabs, the status and the selection. The side column (`SideWidth`, 320 px, dragged by its 1 px splitter between 240 and 900), the content drawer (`ContentHeight`, 318 px) and the Day dock (`DayDockHeight`, 300 px) are resized by dragging their edges (`HeightGrip` for the two docked panels, up to three quarters of the window), and their sizes and the drawer's open state are remembered between runs (`EditorLayout`).
- The content browser is a drawer: closed it gives its height to the viewport; Ctrl+Space or the strip's Content tab opens it (`MainWindowViewModel.IsContentOpen`). Its header holds the folder's trail, the search, the category chips (`AssetTypes.Filters`: All, Scenes, Art, Light, Data, Code, each an `AssetFilter` of several types) and New, Import, Refresh and Close; files are 104 px tiles with a thumbnail or the type's icon over the type's colour bar, the name and the kind, folders dashed tiles.
- Ctrl+K or the top bar's command box opens the command palette under the top bar (`SearchMenu.ShowPalette`, the searchable menu 600 px wide with `MenuGrouping.Sections`: groups under headings, kept while searching): the commands with their keys (`MainWindowViewModel.PaletteChoices`, and Materials and Project settings from the window), and, found only by searching (`MenuChoice.SearchOnly`), every content file (opened as a double-click would, its folder as the `Detail`) and every entity of the level (selected, its outline folder as the detail). Settings' values are not in it yet.
- The inspector (`InspectorView`): the entity's header (its icon, name, place in the outline and template), a vertical category rail at the left (All, then one icon per module the components come from, `CategoryIcons`, `Categories`, `SelectedCategory`; the last button shows the filter box, `FilterText`: a component stays when its name or a field's name matches), and the components as sections, each with its icon and, folded, a summary of its first values (`ComponentViewModel.Icon`, `IsExpanded`, `Summary`); the first three start open and folding is remembered by component name. Each component is a panel card.
- Panel cards (rule R26, owner 2026-11-27: "Create a Panel Card control with the style that is used in the Inspector and reuse it everywhere"): every section of every side panel, creator panel, editor tab and settings page is a `PanelCard` (`Shell/Controls`, a `HeaderedContentControl`): a 24 px header (a chevron, the `Icon`, the title, a faint `Note` such as a count, the card's own `Actions` and, with a `Menu`, its More button) raised while open; a click on the header folds it, and folded the header sinks into the panel and shows the `Summary` of what the card holds (rule R12). Its body holds `PanelRow`s: a `Label` beside its value, the labels of a card sharing one column (`SharedSizeGroup` in the card's scope) 96 px wide or as wide as the longest label, at most 170 px; a row whose value holds rows of its own (`IsNested`: an object, a list) puts the value under the label, indented 10 px. Values follow the panel's width as its edge is dragged; when the panel is too narrow for a value to keep 124 px (`PanelRow.ValueWidth`) beside a long label the label is cut with an ellipsis and its tooltip reads it whole, so the row's own help tooltip shows only over labels that read in full. The data forms (`DataObjectViewModel` and `DataListViewModel`, through `App.axaml`'s templates), the inspector, project settings, the flow, material and texture editors, the vehicle, prop and foliage creators, the Tool tab and the music side panel are all built from them; `EditorThemeTests` fails on a view that builds a section of its own (an `Expander`, a `section` class or a hand-made label column).
- The outliner has a search row (`SceneTreeViewModel.SearchText`: the entities found, without folders), folders with their counts (`SceneTreeItem.Count`) and an eye on each row on hover.
- An opened environment is edited in the Day dock under the viewport (below). Editors open in place (rule R6): the flow, material and texture editors and the project settings are document tabs in the top bar beside the level's (`DocumentTabs`, `MainWindowViewModel.Documents`, each `EditorDocument` with its icon; selecting a tab shows its editor where the viewport was, the level's tab shows the viewport again, and closing a tab forgets its editor, its edits staying held unsaved). The material and texture editors put the preview in the working area with their fields in a 320 px column at the right; the project settings are switches and choices under a category rail. The flow editor follows the design: nodes are cards with a tinted header and icon by what they do (`FlowNodeLook`: Dialogue, Choice, Quest, Start, Finish, Camera, Condition, Effect, tinted by the theme's `Flow<Kind>HeaderBrush` and `Flow<Kind>IconBrush`), a dialogue line showing its speaker in bold over its text, ring pins on the card's edges, wires in muted brown that turn accent (dashed when they loop back) for the selected node, a dotted grid, a pill over the canvas (Add node with Shift+A, fit, zoom in percent) and a minimap (`FlowMinimap`) that centres the view on a click. A document may take the side column while it is shown (`EditorDocument.Side`, `DocumentTabs.HasSide`): the flow's holds the selected node's fields over a Nodes list that selects and frames a node (`FlowGraphViewModel.Reveal`), in place of the context panel and outliner. There is one way to save (rule R21): Ctrl+S and the project menu's Save run `SaveShown`, saving the tab shown when it saves on its own (a material, a texture's settings), the assets for one saved with them (a flow graph), else the scene or the creation; the editors have no Save buttons of their own.

> UI work follows the rules in `Docs/Design/EditorUi.md` (R1 to R26).

- Avalonia for the editor, with its GPU interop for the viewport. The editor is four projects in `Source/Editor`: `Shell` (`FlickerVoid.Editor`, the program: AXAML views, the viewport's GPU interop, the material editor window, the project picker and Play), `Core` (`FlickerVoid.Editor.Core`: `EditorScene`, the camera, gizmos, picking, overlays, and the inspector and View tab view models), `Assets` (`FlickerVoid.Editor.Assets`: the asset browser, material documents and unsaved content) and `Persistence` (`FlickerVoid.Editor.Persistence`: project files, building, creating and launching games). Only the shell uses Avalonia, so the others run headless in tests; it maps Avalonia keys and mouse to Core's `ViewportInput` (`ViewportKeys`). The editor follows MVVM: AXAML views with compiled bindings, view models on CommunityToolkit.Mvvm (`[ObservableProperty]` partial properties), and testable models (`EditorCamera`, `EditorScene`) under them. Its viewport draws the world through `SceneRenderer` with editor passes added after post-processing (`SkyPass`, a sky-like gradient where the scene left the view empty and hidden in the game view, then `GridPass`, thin faint lines that only suggest the ground, then `OverlayPass` for gizmos, selection shapes and the icons of lights and other invisible entities, built each frame by `OverlayBuilder`). When the selection is a camera, its live view is drawn in the viewport's bottom right corner, framed (`ViewportViewModel.GetPreviewCamera`; `ViewportRenderer` draws it with a second `SceneRenderer` without the editor passes and copies it in with `ICommandList.CopyTexture`'s destination offset). Picking is on the CPU, so it needs no GPU: icons in screen space first (`EditorIcons`; camera cues show an outlined camera with a play badge, `CameraCue.png`, unlike cameras), then model triangles (`ScenePicker`, its meshes made by the edited game's `MeshSources`, `EditorScene.Meshes`, so a module's meshes such as a creation's parts are picked too; a part picks the creation it belongs to, which the selection box and F bound by its parts). Clicks and drags go to the viewport's mode (`IViewportMode` in Core: pointer handling, a per-frame update, and the undo history and save it acts on): `SceneMode` over the scene (shot keys, the gizmo, then selection) unless the creator is open. The creator (`CreatorMode`, Core `Creators`) opens on a creation placed in the level (`CreatorPlacement.InPlace`, everything else `Greyed`) or on its file alone (`Alone`, a temporary copy at the origin, outside the scene and never saved, everything else `Undrawn`). While it is open only the creation's parts are picked (`ScenePicker.PickPart`; `SelectedPart` holds the clicked part's id), a work light (a point light, never saved) follows the view, parts that a file change spawns for other copies are marked too, and undo, redo and save act on the creation file's history and save it was given; leaving (`Dispose`) takes the marks, the light and the copy away. The shell opens it (`ViewportViewModel.Creator`, `IsCreating`, `CreatorFile`; `MainWindowViewModel.Creator`): a double-click on a placed vehicle (`DoubleClicked`, raising `CreationRequested` with its entity and file) makes it in place, and opening a vehicle file in the asset browser makes it alone. Entering clears the selection and frames the creation; while it is open the scene's icons, selection and gizmo are hidden, and so are the level's outlines and doorways (Rendering draws neither on `Greyed` entities), Delete and Ctrl+D do nothing, F frames the creation, Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y and the top bar's Undo and Redo walk the file's history (`GetHistory` falls back to `Viewport.Mode.History`), Ctrl+S saves the file and never the level, the context panel shows the creator's tab, named and drawn after what is made (`CreatorTab`, `CreatorKind`, `CreatorIcon`; `CreatorPanel` is a `CreatorPanelViewModel`, the base every creator's panel shares for its parts and the mesh tools' way to its file, shown as the `VehiclePanelView` for a vehicle, the `BuildingPanelView` for a building, `Buildings.md`, the `PropPanelView` for a prop, `Props.md`, or the `FoliagePanelView` for a tree or bush, `Foliage.md`, and opened again after an undo, a discard or a change on disk), the outliner the Parts tab (`CreatorParts`; `CreatorPart` is the part selected there and in the viewport alike, `CreatorMode.SelectPart`), whose selected part the overlay outlines (`FindSelectedPart`) and a bar over the viewport shows the file (`CreatorHeading`, `*` while unsaved), Duplicate and Edit and Leave. Duplicate and Edit (only in place) copies the file's group as the asset browser's Duplicate does, moves the unsaved edits to the copy, discards them from the first file and makes the placed entity name the copy, one scene edit. Escape, Leave, a double-click beside the creation, opening another scene and Compile leave the creator; the viewport's options there offer Frame Creation, Leave Creator, Undo and Redo. The outliner still selects the level's entities. Editor icons are Material Symbols (Apache-2.0) PNGs in `Source/Editor/Core/Icons` and the editor's shaders are in `Source/Editor/Core/Shaders`; the shell builds them into `Content/Engine/EditorIcons` and `Content/Engine/Editor`. F frames the selection, or the whole scene, keeping the view's direction (`ViewportViewModel.FrameSelected`, `EditorCamera.Frame`); a right-click without a drag opens the viewport's options. The move and scale gizmos also have a small plane handle in the corner between each two handles (`GizmoAxis.XY`, `YZ`, `XZ`, owner 2026-11-25): a see-through square coloured as the axis it stands across, as in Unity and Godot, that moves on its plane along both axes, or scales both alike by how far the drag goes along their diagonal; the axis handles win where a square shows over them. The gizmo's move and rotate handles follow the world's axes or the entity's own (`GizmoSpace`; scale handles always follow its own), and it snaps when the viewport toolbar turns it on (`GizmoSnap`): moves put the dragged axis (each of a plane's two) on a grid, turns go in steps of degrees, scaling in steps of the starting scale. Menus are shared controls (`Shell/Controls`): view models offer `MenuChoice`s (Core `Menus`: name, group, icon name from `MaterialIcons.Find`, key, action) and each menu picks its own `MenuGrouping` (none, separators or submenus); Shift+A opens the searchable Add menu (`SearchMenu` over `SearchMenuViewModel`, via `EditorMenus.AddChoices`) over the viewport (`EntityPreset`s), the asset browser (`NewChoices`) and the flow graph, and a right-click opens a plain options menu (`EditorMenus.ShowOptions`, `MenuNode`s through the `ChoiceMenuItem` theme); menus scroll past `EditorMenuMaxHeight`. `SearchBox` is the one search field (rounded, magnifying glass, clear button), bound to whatever it searches. The side column (owner, 2026-11-25) holds the context panel over the outliner, every splitter resizing, and the outliner's corner button swaps the two (`SwapPanels`, `ContextRow`, `OutlinerRow`), remembered for the next run in the user's `FlickerVoid/Editor/Layout.json` (`EditorLayout` in Persistence `Layout`). The context panel shows only what has something to show (`ShowsInspector`, `ShowsData`, `ShowsCreator`, `HasContext`; empty, it says how to fill it): the Inspector while an entity is selected, the Data tab while a data asset or table is open (its tab closes it, `CloseData`), and the Vehicle tab while a vehicle is made; a tab that appears comes in front (`SideTab`), and when the front one goes the one shown before it comes back. The outliner holds the Scene and Entity tabs, the View tab (opening a mood in the asset browser brings it in front) and, in the creator, the Parts tab in place of Scene and Entity (`OutlinerTab`). The scene outliner (`SceneTreeViewModel`) shows folders and entities with icons (`SceneIcons`, by component): an entity's folder is `EntityData.Folder` in the scene file (`folder`, held as the Runtime component `EntityFolder` and saved back); choosing a folder selects everything in it, and the selection holds many entities (`EditorSelection.Entities`, with `Selected` for the inspector and gizmo, which moves the rest with it); folders are created (filing the selection), renamed, removed and dragged into each other there. Entities nest under other entities (`EntityData.Parent` in the scene file, `parent`, the parent's name, held as the Runtime component `EntityParent` and saved back; `EditorScene.SetParent`, undone as a `ParentStep`, `GetParent`, `GetChildren`, `GetDescendants`): a child follows its parent's moves, in the editor and the game, through Runtime's `TransformHierarchy` (a game service `Game` updates after each fixed step and before each frame, and `EditorScene` after each edit), which keeps each child's place in its parent's space (`Attachment`, never saved) while transforms stay in world space for every other system. The outliner lists only entities at the top; an entity with nested ones has a reveal button that shows its whole tree under it, the nested names in blue (or every one, with the outliner's reveal toggle; a selection made elsewhere reveals and expands what holds it), an entity dropped on another nests under it and one filed in a folder comes off its parent; the Entity tab beside it (`EntityTreeViewModel`, `EntityTreeView`) shows the selection's whole tree from its top entity, everything nested shown; both views set their tree's items themselves while syncing, as a new item list makes a `TreeView` drop its selection and that must not read as a choice. Each row (`SceneTreeRow`) has an eye that hides the entity and what is nested under it, with the parts of creations, in the editor's views (`EditorScene.SetHidden`, `ShowAll`: Rendering's `Hidden` tag, which `WorldExtractor`, picking, icons and trigger shapes skip; neither saved nor undone, and the game shows them), and the outliner's bar hides or shows the selection and shows everything. The shell's look lives in `Themes/EditorTheme.axaml`: Fluent recoloured with a warm dark palette, never flat: the window, bars, headers and tabs are the frame (`EditorFrameBrush`, the kitchen palette's green), and every content area (viewport, trees, lists, forms, the flow canvas) sits in a `Border.well` sunk into it (the darker panel colour, slightly rounded, an inner shadow), with text inputs darker still; compact sizes, icon buttons (Material Icons path data in `MaterialIcons`) coloured by what they do (`play`, `save`, `delete`, `bake`), and the viewport bars; the main window extends into its title bar (`ExtendClientAreaToDecorationsHint`), so the logo, title, tool buttons and Play share one bar with the system's caption buttons, and the empty bar behind them (`WindowDecorationProperties.ElementRole` TitleBar) drags the window; numbers in the inspector are `DragNumberBox`es, dragged sideways or typed after a click, with a bar in the axis colour on X, Y and Z and a yellow one (`EditorValueBrush`) on every other number. Every edit of the editor's world goes through `EditorScene` (`SetComponent`, `AddComponent`, `RemoveComponent`, `AddEntity`, `DuplicateEntity` (Ctrl+D), `DestroyEntity`), which raises `Edited` and leaves the scene unsaved (`IsUnsaved`, `UnsavedChanged`) until it is saved or another scene opens; the inspector, gizmo drags and Bake Probes' file names all use it, and nothing else writes to that world. Undo and redo (Core `Undo`: `UndoHistory` of `UndoStep`s, one history per document, owner 2026-11-22) store state, never inverse operations: each `EditorScene` edit records the touched component's JSON before and after (`ComponentStep`), a folder change (`FolderStep`) or the whole entity added or deleted (`EntityStep`), all keyed by entity name, since handles die with their entity, and applied back through the same edit methods while `IsApplying` keeps them from recording; `BeginGroup`/`EndGroup` make a gizmo drag, a duplicate or delete of the selection, outliner filing, a number box drag (`DragNumberBox.DragStartedEvent`/`DragCompletedEvent`) or a flow node drag one step, merging repeated edits of the same thing (`TryMerge`); histories keep at most 200 steps and 64 MB. `UnsavedContent.Edit` records each asset edit as the whole file before and after (`ContentStep`, a history per file from `GetHistory`, kept through saves, cleared by a discard, a change on disk or a move or delete), and an undo that reaches the file on disk leaves it saved; `Restored` tells the material editor, the View and Data tabs, flow and texture windows to open the file again. `UndoShortcuts` gives each window Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y and Ctrl+Shift+Z over its document (the main window's View tab's mood while the keyboard is in the outliner on it, the Data tab's file while it is in the context panel on it, else the viewport mode's: the scene, or the creation's file), the top bar's Undo and Redo buttons (`MainWindowViewModel.UndoCommand`, `RedoCommand`, over the history the keyboard is in, `OutlinerFocused`, `SidePanelFocused`) do the same, the viewport passes Ctrl shortcuts on to its window, and the viewport's options name the next steps. Selection, camera moves and asset browser file operations are not undone. Every file the editor edits as an object is a `ContentDocument<T>` (`MaterialDocument`, `MoodDocument`, `FlowDocument`, `DataDocument`, `TableDocument`, `VehicleDocument`): it reads the file's current contents, unsaved edits included, into an object nothing else holds, and `Hold` writes it back as one undo step. Material and mood edits are held unsaved by `UnsavedContent`, the `IContentManager` the editor runs on, which serves them to whatever loads through it (the viewport shows them at once) until they are saved or discarded; a file that changes on disk while it has unsaved edits keeps them, and the status bar says so. The title shows `*` while anything is unsaved, and the unsaved assets prompt (`UnsavedAssetsDialog`: Save, Discard, Cancel) lists the scene and the assets before the editor closes, and the scene before another scene opens (`MainWindowViewModel.AskUnsaved`). The Material Editor is its own window (`MaterialEditorWindow`): a browser of the content folder's `.fvmat` files as a tree under the masters and materials they are instances of (`MaterialBrowser.FindTree`, `MaterialTreeNode`; a search keeps a match's parents; every master of the content is listed, the engine's included), New instance making one of the master or material picked (`Surface Instance.fvmat`, keeping all its values), the material's parent picked among the content's materials and masters (`MaterialParentValue`; changing it opens the material again), and a row per parameter the scene shader and then the material's master declare (`MaterialLineage`: the values it keeps from the nearest parent setting each, and its master), each a field (the inspector's field view models over `IEditableValue`) with an override tick, as in Unreal's instance editor (`MaterialParameterViewModel` over `MaterialParameterValue`: unticked shows the parent's value or the default, greyed, with where it comes from; editing or ticking copies it into the material, unticking takes it out), edits held unsaved, saved with Ctrl+S or dropped with Discard, a summary of a master picked in the tree (its shading model and parameters), and a sphere, cube or plane preview (`PreviewView`: the shape, its turn, a `PreviewMood` of Day, Night (faint blue light and a warm lamp) or Indoors (a warm lamp overhead, no sun), and the light's turn about the shape; each mood reflects a public domain panorama of the engine's, `Engine/Rendering/Reflections`: Day a sunny quarry, Night a moonless golf course, Indoors a warm sunset; a drag turns the shape, a right- or Shift-drag the light; it fills the working area and renders at its size, in steps of 32 pixels from 128 to 1024, `ResizePreview`) rendered by `MaterialPreview` on its own device over the editor's content (its meshes and material in a hidden `.material-preview` folder, so the material's textures are found) and read back into a bitmap, so it needs no GPU interop. The inspector has no per-component code: it lists the components registered for scene files (`ISceneSpawner.Components`, each a `SceneComponent` with its source-generated `JsonTypeInfo`) and picks a field view model by property type, refined by optional attributes; Add Component opens the searchable Add menu of the missing components grouped by module with icons (`ComponentChoices`) (`[Color]` and `[ContentFile]` from Runtime; `[Range]` from DataAnnotations); string arrays such as `MeshRenderer.Materials` are edited item by item (`ListFieldViewModel`); other lists and arrays (a shot's keys, an area's tags) get a field per item (`CollectionFieldViewModel` over `ElementValue`s) and nested objects (a cue's transition) a field per property (`ObjectFieldViewModel` over `MemberValue`s), with a kind to switch for a polymorphic one (a body's collider), every edit writing a copy back through the owner (`EditableJson`) so undo keeps the old value; each field's row (`FieldRow` in the shell's `App.axaml`) puts nested ones under their label; tags are edited as their names (`TagProperty`). A string naming a content file (`[ContentFile]`) is picked like Unreal's asset picker (`AssetFieldViewModel`, and each item of such a list, over `AssetPickerViewModel`; `AssetPickerView` in the shell): the current file with its kind's icon opens a popup listing the files with the field's extensions, read again on each opening, with a search box (Enter takes the first match) and None when the value may be empty; the button beside it raises `AssetPickerView.OpenRequestedEvent`, and the main window (for its own fields and the flow editor's) shows the file in the asset browser (`AssetBrowserViewModel.Reveal`) and opens it as a double-click would (`MainWindowViewModel.OpenContentFile`). The outliner's View tab edits the viewport's `LightingMood` the same way (`ViewSettingsViewModel`, a field per mood property): the mood file the open scene names (a `SceneMood` component on an entity, from Rendering; the Add menu's Scene Mood), or else a `.fvmood` opened in the asset browser, or else the default mood, unsaved; the viewport draws with that file and the View tab edits it (`MoodDocument`, its edits held by `UnsavedContent` like a material's). It layers them as the game does (`ViewportViewModel.GetLayeredMood`): the lens the main camera names, then the values the mood changes from the defaults, so a canvas or tonemapper picked in the View tab shows over the lens's; the tab's Canvas checkbox (`ShowCanvas`) hides the canvas in the viewport only, and Play keeps it. The mood includes its `Tonemapper`: post-processing ends with a tonemapper picked by enum (`Uncharted2` Hable filmic by default, `Aces`, `None`) and switched in `Post.hlsl`'s `Tonemap`; a new one is an enum value and a case there. Bake Probes (`SceneProbes`) bakes every irradiance volume with that mood on a device of its own into its probes file in the content folder (a volume without one gets `<Scene>.<Volume>.fvprobes` beside the scene), and the viewport's probe cache reloads it. Baking traces rays, so it needs a GPU with ray queries (the status says so otherwise); the viewport itself lights its scene's first volume with live probes while it traces rays, and the file serves stages, previews and GPUs without ray queries. Save (Ctrl+S) writes the world back into the open scene file (`SceneLoader.Save` over `ISceneSpawner.Capture`): one entity per line, components the world left unchanged kept as the file wrote them and changed ones written in full; comments are lost. Save Assets (Ctrl+Shift+S) and Discard Assets save or drop every unsaved material and mood. The asset browser under the viewport (`AssetBrowserViewModel` over `AssetCatalog`) shows the content folder's tree under its search box, a narrow column of type filters and the selected folder's subfolders and files as tiles (`Items`, an icon and colour per type, `AssetTypeLooks`); each type of file is one `AssetType` in `AssetTypes` (its name, extensions, icon, colour and what New writes, `AssetNew`), so a new type of file is one entry there, and the main window opens each type in its editor from one table (`MainWindowViewModel.CreateOpeners`), hidden dot-files skipped; the search looks through everything below the selected folder; it has no buttons: tiles and tree folders have context menus (open, rename, duplicate, delete, new, import, reimport, up, refresh), and a tile dropped on a folder moves there (`MoveTo`); files from outside the editor, dropped on the browser or picked with Import, are copied into the folder they land on or the selected one (`Import`), their sources remembered in the content folder's hidden `.imports` file (`ImportSources`, kept through renames, moves and deletes; the builder skips hidden files and Blender files), so Reimport copies a changed source again; opening a scene replaces the open one (`EditorScene.Open`, `SceneLoader.Unload`), opening a material shows it in the material editor, and a model dragged into the viewport becomes a mesh entity named after it, and a vehicle file a vehicle (`EntityPreset.ForVehicle`), on the mesh or ground under the mouse (`AssetDrag`, `ViewportViewModel.DropAsset`). It creates folders and the files of every type New makes (scenes, vehicles, materials, moods, canvases and flow graphs; a new vehicle is a car on four wheels), named `New Scene` and so on, numbered when taken, naming the content folder's schema, and renames and deletes files and folders, all through `IContentManager` (`Write`, `CreateFolder`, `Move`, `Delete`). A file owns the files in its folder named after it (`AssetCatalog`): a creation (`AssetType.IsCreation`, such as `Van.fvvehicle`) every `Van.<anything>` (its `.blend`, models and paintings), any other file those named after its whole name (an image's `Paper.png.fvtexture` settings), the shortest name winning, so a group's files all start with its name. The browser shows a group as its owner's tile, framed in its type's colour, whose arrow lists the owned files after it (`ToggleExpanded`; `AssetEntry.Owned`, `Owner`, `Label`); renaming, moving and duplicating any of its tiles act on the whole group (Duplicate, Ctrl+D, numbers the copy from 2, a name none of the group's files takes), while Delete takes the whole group from the owner's tile and one file from an owned tile; a type filter lists a matching owned file on its own, `Reveal` expands the group of an owned file, and asset pickers list owned files too (`ContentFileFinder`). With a project open it also shows the project's `Source` folder as a second root, Code (`AssetCatalog.ForCode`, paths under `/Code`, build output skipped, `.cs` files opened in the IDE), where it only writes: New > Flow > Flow Node asks for a name and a code folder (`FlowNodePrompt`: the selected code folder, or the one whose path ends most like the selected content folder's, or the first holding a C# project) and `FlowNodeSource` writes the class there in its project's namespace and adds it to the project's JSON context; it is in flow graphs after the next build. Data assets open in the Data tab (`DataDocument` over `DataTypes.ReadAsset`/`WriteAsset`, edits held by `UnsavedContent`): a form generated from the asset's metadata (`DataObjectViewModel`: a field per value, a section per nested object, and a `DataListViewModel` per list of objects such as fragments, whose Add offers each `$type` registered for the element type); the browser's New Data Asset and New Table submenus list the registered types (`AssetChoice`). Tables open there too (`TableDocument`, `TableViewModel`): a form per row, rows added, renamed and removed, and CSV export and import for spreadsheets (`TableCsv`: a `name` column, then one per property, strings as written and other values as JSON). Every save goes through `IContentManager.Write`, which in a development build also writes the source file the build manifest maps it to, so editor changes outlive the next build.
- Whole trees: duplicating an entity copies everything nested under it (taken names numbered, save ids given free ones, the copies nested under their copied parents), deleting removes the tree deepest first, and the viewport skips selected entities nested under other selected ones. F2 or the outliner's Rename renames an entity (`EditorScene.RenameEntity`, undone as a `RenameStep`), refusing a taken name and rewriting every `[EntityReference]` that names it. Save as Template (the outliner's menu) writes the entity as `Templates/<Name>.fventity` and has it start from it, and the inspector's Template row picks the template an entity starts from (`EditorScene.SetTemplate`, a `TemplateStep`). Nested entities are not part of templates yet.

Project settings (owner, 2026-11-25): the gear in the title bar, before Open in Visual Studio, opens the project settings in a document tab over the viewport (`ProjectSettingsView` over Core's `ProjectSettingsViewModel`). Its Graphics page lists the edited game's saved rendering console variables (`r.`, Rendering.md), each named for people from its name (`r.RayTracing` is "Ray tracing", `SettingViewModel.Label`) with its help, name and default as the tooltip: a check box for a boolean (`SwitchSettingViewModel`), a list for named values (`ChoiceSettingViewModel`) and a text box otherwise (`TextSettingViewModel`). - Level greyboxing (milestone 6 of the creation tools): the level's own greyboxes are shaped with the same mesh tools, in place. A double-click on a greybox, or the viewport's Shape button while one is selected (`ViewportViewModel.ShapeSelected`, `PieceRequested`), enters `CreatorMode.Piece`: the creator's mode over the greybox itself (`CreatorPlacement.Piece`; its only part is itself, `PieceId`, always selected), with the rest of the level greyed and the mesh entered at once. Its mesh is the greybox's `mesh`, or the box of its size until the first edit (`PieceShape`); every edit sets the greybox component, so undo, redo and save are the level's, and there is no panel, mirror or door tool. Escape leaves the mesh, then the greybox. With several greyboxes selected the Cut button (`CutSelected`, `PieceTools.Cut`) takes the others out of the one selected last where they overlap it, with Booleans, as they stand in the level: a tunnel through a hill, a doorway, a window. The cutters stay where they are, and a cutter that takes nothing away changes nothing. The inspector shows a shape as its point and face counts.

Mesh mode (milestone 2 of the creation tools, `Core/Meshes`): in a creator `EnterMesh()` (Tab, wired in the shell by task 2.15) enters the mesh of the selected part, `LeaveMesh()` leaves it; selecting another part leaves it too. The creator is given `findMesh`, which gives a part's solid by id (the shell passes the vehicle panel's). While a mesh is entered a click selects in it and no longer picks parts. `MeshSelection` holds the selected points, edges and faces of the solid as in Blender: `Element` is the kind a click selects (1, 2, 3), the other kinds follow from the selection (a point selection holds the edges and faces whose points are all selected, edges give their points and the faces whose edges are all selected, faces give their edges and points), changing the kind keeps what follows, Shift toggles, a click on nothing clears, `SelectAll` and `Clear`, and `SelectLoop` (Alt) takes the edge loop through an edge, or the strip of faces across the nearest edge of a face (`SolidTopology`, `Scenes.md`). `MeshPicker` finds the element under the mouse on the CPU as `ScenePicker` does: a face by the ray, an edge or a point within 10 pixels on screen, and only edges and points that touch a face turned toward the camera; a solid hiding another part of itself is not tested. A file change that gives the part a new solid keeps the mesh entered and starts the selection again, empty, in the same kind. Drawing the selection comes in the next task, and the tools after it.

The mesh overlay (`MeshOverlay`, drawn by `CreatorMode.DrawMesh` in place of the part's outline while a mesh is entered) shows the edges of the faces turned toward the camera, the points of those faces in point mode, the selected faces filled, the selected edges and points in the selection colour and the element under the mouse (`CreatorMode.Hover`, found like a click).

Editing the entered mesh: `CreatorMode.MoveMesh`, `RotateMesh` and `ScaleMesh` act on the selected points (in the part's own space; rotation and scale turn about their middle, `MeshEdit`), give the part a new `SolidMesh` through `VehicleDocument.SetMesh` and so hold it as one undo step in the file's history, which placed copies see through hot reload; the selection follows the new solid while it has the same faces (`MeshSelection.TryFollow`), also after an undo. `MeshTransformBar` is the numeric bar's model: a tool and the typed values, applied to the selection and reset.

Extrude (`MeshExtrude.Faces`, `CreatorMode.ExtrudeMesh`): the selected faces, which in point and edge mode are the faces the selection holds whole, are pushed by a distance along their average normal as one region, with a wall of new faces round its border; points the region has all round go. The pushed faces stay selected (face mode) to be moved or extruded again. Nothing selected, or every face, does nothing. Extruding edges and points would leave the solid open, so it is not offered.

Inset (`MeshInset.Faces`, `CreatorMode.InsetMesh`): the selected faces' border is pulled in within the faces by a thickness (a corner is mitred so both edges move the thickness) and a ring of new faces joins it to the faces round; points inside the region stay, and the inner faces stay selected. A thickness the faces can't take turns them inside out; nothing checks it. The inset has no depth: push the inner faces with Extrude or Move.

Loop cut (`MeshLoopCut.Cut`, `CreatorMode.LoopCutMesh`, for L, or Ctrl+R): across the ring of quads through the edge nearest the mouse in the face under it, N loops are added, evenly spaced and moved together by a slide from -1 to 1; each ring edge gets N points and each quad of the ring is split along them. A ring that ends at a face that is not a quad leaves it with the extra points on its edge. One undo step; the new edges stay selected. The drag that slides and the wheel that sets N come with the shell (2.15); sliding while dragging will need the shell to fold its repeated calls into one history step.

Bevel (`MeshBevel.Edges`, `CreatorMode.BevelMesh`, for B, or Ctrl+B): each selected edge becomes strips of faces, a width on each face, in N rows, flat or round (a curve toward the old edge). Where several selected edges meet at a point, each run of faces between two of them gets one point where their offsets meet and the strips are joined by a cap face; where one ends at a point with three edges the strip ends on the two edges beside it and the face between them is cut across. A strip ending at a point with more than three edges is refused (nothing changes), as are points where the faces do not close into one fan. Faces are never checked for the width fitting. One undo step; the strips stay selected.

Hollow (`MeshHollow.Shell`, `MeshDragTool.Hollow`, for H, owner 2026-11-27): the entered mesh becomes a shell at once, with walls of the Tool tab's thickness (Wall), pending until Enter or Apply; the mouse does not set it: a copy with every face's plane moved in by the thickness (each point solved by least squares where its faces' planes meet again) is subtracted with Manifold. Selected faces are left open: their planes move out instead, so the inside breaks through them (a box with its top selected becomes an open box). Needs no selection; a wall thicker than half the solid gives nothing.

Mirror (`CreatorMode.Mirror`, `SetMirror`, `MeshMirror`): with the part's switch on, an edit on one side is made on the other too. A point's twin is the point at the same height and depth across the X = 0 plane. Move, Rotate and Scale carry the result to the twins of the moved points that were not moved themselves and keep points on the plane on it; Extrude, Inset and Bevel work on the selection with its twin faces or edges added (Extrude pushes each separate run of faces along its own normal); a loop cut also cuts the ring across, unless it is the same ring. Parts without twins (a lopsided mesh) are edited on the side picked only. The switch is a `VehicleDocument.SetMirror` undo step saved in the file.

Mesh Booleans (`SolidBoolean.Combine`, `Meshes/Booleans`): two solids in one space are added, subtracted or intersected by Manifold (Apache-2.0), through its C binding `manifoldc`, which `Source/Native/Manifold/build.sh` or `build.ps1` builds from source into `artifacts/native/<rid>` (git, CMake and a C++ compiler; CI builds it before testing), run by the first build of Editor Core that finds it missing (`Source/Native/Native.targets`; `FlickerVoidBuildNative=false` turns that off). `ManifoldNative` finds the library beside the program or in `artifacts/native` above it. The result's coplanar triangles that make a convex quad are joined again; the rest stay triangles, and a face with a hole stays triangles too. Where the library is not built, `SolidBoolean.IsAvailable` is false and its tests skip, unless `FLICKERVOID_REQUIRE_MANIFOLD=1`, which CI sets.

Cut (`MeshCut.Faces`, `CreatorMode.CutMesh`, for C): the selected faces are cut into the solid, as a pocket to a depth (a negative extrude, so its floor stays selected) or through it: a prism of each face, running the solid's length inward and a little outward, is subtracted with Manifold (`SolidBoolean`), so the faces must be convex and nothing is selected after. An opening smaller than its face is made by an inset first. A through cut returns nothing where Manifold's library is not built.

Door tool (owner, 2026-11-27, `DoorFitter`, `DoorSession`, `CreatorMode.BeginDoor`, for D, also the rail's Door item; replaces the door cut from a face): a placed door, kept live. With the tool in hand a door follows the mouse onto the wall under it, standing on the wall's lowest point and turned to the face's normal (`DoorSession.Aim`, upright faces only); a click places it, and nothing is cut before Apply. The red box is the cut (everything of the wall inside it goes), with a red square on its front and one behind that move that side along the normal only (`DoorSession.BeginDrag`; the Tool tab holds the same numbers, and Through cuts the whole wall); the green arcs are the hinge's sweep at the door's foot and head, the grab point is a small sphere, and a second red box is the window that cuts the door itself (with an optional pane of glass); a frame is a ring round the opening. All numbers are a `DoorFit` (in the wall part's space) on the door part, and the wall keeps its uncut solid in `SolidPart.Source`: `DoorFitter.Rebuild` cuts every placed door's box from the source with Manifold, adds the frames and makes each door's slab (minus its window) and hinge (a `PartJoint` in the creation's space whose axis swings the free edge out of the face, or into the wall, by the hinge side and Outward), so a door can be selected again (a click on its part opens its fit with Apply, Cancel and Remove door), moved or changed, and removing it closes the wall. Edits to the wall's mesh itself make its cuts final (`DoorFitter.Bake`: the doors stay as plain doors). One undo step through `CreationDocument.PlaceDoor`, `ReplaceDoor` and `RemoveDoor` (the creator reaches them through `IDoorHost`). A door's use point is its grab point. `CreatorMode.SwingDoor` opens or shuts a door at once to see it swing. The cuts need Manifold's library; without it the rail still shows Door (in every creator but a level piece, `HasDoorTool`), and using it, or Hollow, says to run `Source/Native/Manifold/build.ps1` (owner 2026-11-27: the tool had vanished on a PC without the library). Doors made the old way, from a face, stay in files as plain doors.

### The music editor (owner, 2026-11-27)

A `.fvmusic` file opens in a document tab (`MusicEditorView`, `Editor.Core/Music`) and takes the side column (`EditorDocument.Side`). The owner's words (2026-11-27): the file is an arrangement (it holds ambience as well as music), its rows are layers and the points on its timeline are cues (`MusicCue`, `MusicTrack` and `MusicNotify` in the code). The transport: play (Space), which turns into pause while playing, stop (twice returns to the start), loop, the position as bar.beat.sixteenth and time, Tempo, Meter, Phrase and Snap, and "N of M layers sounding"; zoom out and in, and Fit, which fits every bar into the lanes' width (`FitTo`). An arrangement without a tempo is shown on the hours of the day (the ruler says what it counts, `RulerText`), and its transport has Day runs (`DayPace`: 10 s an hour, the default, 2 s an hour, or Held): while it plays the playhead runs the day at that pace and the preview's clock follows it (`MusicPreview.SetHour`), stopping at the day's end unless it loops. Playing runs `MusicPreview`, a small game of its own with the audio on the system's output (SDL's dummy device, or none, in the cloud), over the arrangement as it is edited. The lanes: the ruler with the playhead (click or drag to jump; playing, the music jumps there), a Rehearsal row for each music area of the open scene that asks for the requested layers (or a script row for a layer nothing asks for), each with a span to drag: while the playhead is inside it the area's layers are requested, so fills and waits are heard; the Cues row (a click picks a cue, a double-click adds one at the bar clicked, a drag moves it; a cue is drawn once at its bar, or at every phrase when it repeats); and a lane per layer with its clips: loops with their waveform, hatched fills, dashed waits, dots for scatter marks, laid out by `MusicArrangement.Plan` with the same `MusicGrid` rule `MusicSystem` uses.
