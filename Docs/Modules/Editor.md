# The editor

> UI work follows the rules in `Docs/Design/EditorUi.md` (R1 to R24).

- Avalonia for the editor, with its GPU interop for the viewport. The editor is four projects in `Source/Editor`: `Shell` (`FlickerVoid.Editor`, the program: AXAML views, the viewport's GPU interop, the material editor window, the project picker and Play), `Core` (`FlickerVoid.Editor.Core`: `EditorScene`, the camera, gizmos, picking, overlays, and the inspector and View tab view models), `Assets` (`FlickerVoid.Editor.Assets`: the asset browser, material documents and unsaved content) and `Persistence` (`FlickerVoid.Editor.Persistence`: project files, building, creating and launching games). Only the shell uses Avalonia, so the others run headless in tests; it maps Avalonia keys and mouse to Core's `ViewportInput` (`ViewportKeys`). The editor follows MVVM: AXAML views with compiled bindings, view models on CommunityToolkit.Mvvm (`[ObservableProperty]` partial properties), and testable models (`EditorCamera`, `EditorScene`) under them. Its viewport draws the world through `SceneRenderer` with editor passes added after post-processing (`SkyPass`, a sky-like gradient where the scene left the view empty and hidden in the game view, then `GridPass`, thin faint lines that only suggest the ground, then `OverlayPass` for gizmos, selection shapes and the icons of lights and other invisible entities, built each frame by `OverlayBuilder`). When the selection is a camera, its live view is drawn in the viewport's bottom right corner, framed (`ViewportViewModel.GetPreviewCamera`; `ViewportRenderer` draws it with a second `SceneRenderer` without the editor passes and copies it in with `ICommandList.CopyTexture`'s destination offset). Picking is on the CPU, so it needs no GPU: icons in screen space first (`EditorIcons`; camera cues show an outlined camera with a play badge, `CameraCue.png`, unlike cameras), then model triangles (`ScenePicker`, its meshes made by the edited game's `MeshSources`, `EditorScene.Meshes`, so a module's meshes such as a creation's parts are picked too; a part picks the creation it belongs to, which the selection box and F bound by its parts). Clicks and drags go to the viewport's mode (`IViewportMode` in Core: pointer handling, a per-frame update, and the undo history and save it acts on): `SceneMode` over the scene (shot keys, the gizmo, then selection) unless the creator is open. The creator (`CreatorMode`, Core `Creators`) opens on a creation placed in the level (`CreatorPlacement.InPlace`, everything else `Greyed`) or on its file alone (`Alone`, a temporary copy at the origin, outside the scene and never saved, everything else `Undrawn`). While it is open only the creation's parts are picked (`ScenePicker.PickPart`; `SelectedPart` holds the clicked part's id), a work light (a point light, never saved) follows the view, parts that a file change spawns for other copies are marked too, and undo, redo and save act on the creation file's history and save it was given; leaving (`Dispose`) takes the marks, the light and the copy away. The shell opens it (`ViewportViewModel.Creator`, `IsCreating`, `CreatorFile`; `MainWindowViewModel.Creator`): a double-click on a placed vehicle (`DoubleClicked`, raising `CreationRequested` with its entity and file) makes it in place, and opening a vehicle file in the asset browser makes it alone. Entering clears the selection and frames the creation; while it is open the scene's icons, selection and gizmo are hidden, and so are the level's outlines and doorways (Rendering draws neither on `Greyed` entities), Delete and Ctrl+D do nothing, F frames the creation, Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y and the top bar's Undo and Redo walk the file's history (`GetHistory` falls back to `Viewport.Mode.History`), Ctrl+S saves the file and never the level, the context panel shows the Vehicle tab (`CreatorTab`, the `VehiclePanelView` over `CreatorPanel`, opened again after an undo, a discard or a change on disk), the outliner the Parts tab (`CreatorParts`; `CreatorPart` is the part selected there and in the viewport alike, `CreatorMode.SelectPart`), whose selected part the overlay outlines (`FindSelectedPart`) and a bar over the viewport shows the file (`CreatorHeading`, `*` while unsaved), Duplicate and Edit and Leave. Duplicate and Edit (only in place) copies the file's group as the asset browser's Duplicate does, moves the unsaved edits to the copy, discards them from the first file and makes the placed entity name the copy, one scene edit. Escape, Leave, a double-click beside the creation, opening another scene and Compile leave the creator; the viewport's options there offer Frame Creation, Leave Creator, Undo and Redo. The outliner still selects the level's entities. Editor icons are Material Symbols (Apache-2.0) PNGs in `Source/Editor/Core/Icons` and the editor's shaders are in `Source/Editor/Core/Shaders`; the shell builds them into `Content/Engine/EditorIcons` and `Content/Engine/Editor`. F frames the selection, or the whole scene, keeping the view's direction (`ViewportViewModel.FrameSelected`, `EditorCamera.Frame`); a right-click without a drag opens the viewport's options. The move and scale gizmos also have a small plane handle in the corner between each two handles (`GizmoAxis.XY`, `YZ`, `XZ`, owner 2026-11-25): a see-through square coloured as the axis it stands across, as in Unity and Godot, that moves on its plane along both axes, or scales both alike by how far the drag goes along their diagonal; the axis handles win where a square shows over them. The gizmo's move and rotate handles follow the world's axes or the entity's own (`GizmoSpace`; scale handles always follow its own), and it snaps when the viewport toolbar turns it on (`GizmoSnap`): moves put the dragged axis (each of a plane's two) on a grid, turns go in steps of degrees, scaling in steps of the starting scale. Menus are shared controls (`Shell/Controls`): view models offer `MenuChoice`s (Core `Menus`: name, group, icon name from `MaterialIcons.Find`, key, action) and each menu picks its own `MenuGrouping` (none, separators or submenus); Shift+A opens the searchable Add menu (`SearchMenu` over `SearchMenuViewModel`, via `EditorMenus.AddChoices`) over the viewport (`EntityPreset`s), the asset browser (`NewChoices`) and the flow graph, and a right-click opens a plain options menu (`EditorMenus.ShowOptions`, `MenuNode`s through the `ChoiceMenuItem` theme); menus scroll past `EditorMenuMaxHeight`. `SearchBox` is the one search field (rounded, magnifying glass, clear button), bound to whatever it searches. The side column (owner, 2026-11-25) holds the context panel over the outliner, every splitter resizing, and the outliner's corner button swaps the two (`SwapPanels`, `ContextRow`, `OutlinerRow`), remembered for the next run in the user's `FlickerVoid/Editor/Layout.json` (`EditorLayout` in Persistence `Layout`). The context panel shows only what has something to show (`ShowsInspector`, `ShowsData`, `ShowsCreator`, `HasContext`; empty, it says how to fill it): the Inspector while an entity is selected, the Data tab while a data asset or table is open (its tab closes it, `CloseData`), and the Vehicle tab while a vehicle is made; a tab that appears comes in front (`SideTab`), and when the front one goes the one shown before it comes back. The outliner holds the Scene and Entity tabs, the View tab (opening a mood in the asset browser brings it in front) and, in the creator, the Parts tab in place of Scene and Entity (`OutlinerTab`). The scene outliner (`SceneTreeViewModel`) shows folders and entities with icons (`SceneIcons`, by component): an entity's folder is `EntityData.Folder` in the scene file (`folder`, held as the Runtime component `EntityFolder` and saved back); choosing a folder selects everything in it, and the selection holds many entities (`EditorSelection.Entities`, with `Selected` for the inspector and gizmo, which moves the rest with it); folders are created (filing the selection), renamed, removed and dragged into each other there. Entities nest under other entities (`EntityData.Parent` in the scene file, `parent`, the parent's name, held as the Runtime component `EntityParent` and saved back; `EditorScene.SetParent`, undone as a `ParentStep`, `GetParent`, `GetChildren`, `GetDescendants`): a child follows its parent's moves, in the editor and the game, through Runtime's `TransformHierarchy` (a game service `Game` updates after each fixed step and before each frame, and `EditorScene` after each edit), which keeps each child's place in its parent's space (`Attachment`, never saved) while transforms stay in world space for every other system. The outliner lists only entities at the top; an entity with nested ones has a reveal button that shows its whole tree under it, the nested names in blue (or every one, with the outliner's reveal toggle; a selection made elsewhere reveals and expands what holds it), an entity dropped on another nests under it and one filed in a folder comes off its parent; the Entity tab beside it (`EntityTreeViewModel`, `EntityTreeView`) shows the selection's whole tree from its top entity, everything nested shown; both views set their tree's items themselves while syncing, as a new item list makes a `TreeView` drop its selection and that must not read as a choice. Each row (`SceneTreeRow`) has an eye that hides the entity and what is nested under it, with the parts of creations, in the editor's views (`EditorScene.SetHidden`, `ShowAll`: Rendering's `Hidden` tag, which `WorldExtractor`, picking, icons and trigger shapes skip; neither saved nor undone, and the game shows them), and the outliner's bar hides or shows the selection and shows everything. The shell's look lives in `Themes/EditorTheme.axaml`: Fluent recoloured with a warm dark palette, never flat: the window, bars, headers and tabs are the frame (`EditorFrameBrush`, the kitchen palette's green), and every content area (viewport, trees, lists, forms, the flow canvas) sits in a `Border.well` sunk into it (the darker panel colour, slightly rounded, an inner shadow), with text inputs darker still; compact sizes, icon buttons (Material Icons path data in `MaterialIcons`) coloured by what they do (`play`, `save`, `delete`, `bake`), and the viewport bars; the main window extends into its title bar (`ExtendClientAreaToDecorationsHint`), so the logo, title, tool buttons and Play share one bar with the system's caption buttons, and the empty bar behind them (`WindowDecorationProperties.ElementRole` TitleBar) drags the window; numbers in the inspector are `DragNumberBox`es, dragged sideways or typed after a click, with a bar in the axis colour on X, Y and Z and a yellow one (`EditorValueBrush`) on every other number. Every edit of the editor's world goes through `EditorScene` (`SetComponent`, `AddComponent`, `RemoveComponent`, `AddEntity`, `DuplicateEntity` (Ctrl+D), `DestroyEntity`), which raises `Edited` and leaves the scene unsaved (`IsUnsaved`, `UnsavedChanged`) until it is saved or another scene opens; the inspector, gizmo drags and Bake Probes' file names all use it, and nothing else writes to that world. Undo and redo (Core `Undo`: `UndoHistory` of `UndoStep`s, one history per document, owner 2026-11-22) store state, never inverse operations: each `EditorScene` edit records the touched component's JSON before and after (`ComponentStep`), a folder change (`FolderStep`) or the whole entity added or deleted (`EntityStep`), all keyed by entity name, since handles die with their entity, and applied back through the same edit methods while `IsApplying` keeps them from recording; `BeginGroup`/`EndGroup` make a gizmo drag, a duplicate or delete of the selection, outliner filing, a number box drag (`DragNumberBox.DragStartedEvent`/`DragCompletedEvent`) or a flow node drag one step, merging repeated edits of the same thing (`TryMerge`); histories keep at most 200 steps and 64 MB. `UnsavedContent.Edit` records each asset edit as the whole file before and after (`ContentStep`, a history per file from `GetHistory`, kept through saves, cleared by a discard, a change on disk or a move or delete), and an undo that reaches the file on disk leaves it saved; `Restored` tells the material editor, the View and Data tabs, flow and texture windows to open the file again. `UndoShortcuts` gives each window Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y and Ctrl+Shift+Z over its document (the main window's View tab's mood while the keyboard is in the outliner on it, the Data tab's file while it is in the context panel on it, else the viewport mode's: the scene, or the creation's file), the top bar's Undo and Redo buttons (`MainWindowViewModel.UndoCommand`, `RedoCommand`, over the history the keyboard is in, `OutlinerFocused`, `SidePanelFocused`) do the same, the viewport passes Ctrl shortcuts on to its window, and the viewport's options name the next steps. Selection, camera moves and asset browser file operations are not undone. Every file the editor edits as an object is a `ContentDocument<T>` (`MaterialDocument`, `MoodDocument`, `FlowDocument`, `DataDocument`, `TableDocument`, `VehicleDocument`): it reads the file's current contents, unsaved edits included, into an object nothing else holds, and `Hold` writes it back as one undo step. Material and mood edits are held unsaved by `UnsavedContent`, the `IContentManager` the editor runs on, which serves them to whatever loads through it (the viewport shows them at once) until they are saved or discarded; a file that changes on disk while it has unsaved edits keeps them, and the status bar says so. The title shows `*` while anything is unsaved, and the unsaved assets prompt (`UnsavedAssetsDialog`: Save, Discard, Cancel) lists the scene and the assets before the editor closes, and the scene before another scene opens (`MainWindowViewModel.AskUnsaved`). The Material Editor is its own window (`MaterialEditorWindow`): a browser of the content folder's `.fvmat` files, a field per parameter the scene shader declares (the inspector's field view models over `IEditableValue`), edits held unsaved with Save and Discard, and a sphere or cube preview rendered by `MaterialPreview` on its own device over the editor's content (its meshes and material in a hidden `.material-preview` folder, so the material's textures are found) and read back into a bitmap, so it needs no GPU interop. The inspector has no per-component code: it lists the components registered for scene files (`ISceneSpawner.Components`, each a `SceneComponent` with its source-generated `JsonTypeInfo`) and picks a field view model by property type, refined by optional attributes; Add Component opens the searchable Add menu of the missing components grouped by module with icons (`ComponentChoices`) (`[Color]` and `[ContentFile]` from Runtime; `[Range]` from DataAnnotations); string arrays such as `MeshRenderer.Materials` are edited item by item (`ListFieldViewModel`); other lists and arrays (a shot's keys, an area's tags) get a field per item (`CollectionFieldViewModel` over `ElementValue`s) and nested objects (a cue's transition) a field per property (`ObjectFieldViewModel` over `MemberValue`s), with a kind to switch for a polymorphic one (a body's collider), every edit writing a copy back through the owner (`EditableJson`) so undo keeps the old value; each field's row (`FieldRow` in the shell's `App.axaml`) puts nested ones under their label; tags are edited as their names (`TagProperty`). A string naming a content file (`[ContentFile]`) is picked like Unreal's asset picker (`AssetFieldViewModel`, and each item of such a list, over `AssetPickerViewModel`; `AssetPickerView` in the shell): the current file with its kind's icon opens a popup listing the files with the field's extensions, read again on each opening, with a search box (Enter takes the first match) and None when the value may be empty; the button beside it raises `AssetPickerView.OpenRequestedEvent`, and the main window (for its own fields and the flow editor's) shows the file in the asset browser (`AssetBrowserViewModel.Reveal`) and opens it as a double-click would (`MainWindowViewModel.OpenContentFile`). The outliner's View tab edits the viewport's `LightingMood` the same way (`ViewSettingsViewModel`, a field per mood property): the mood file the open scene names (a `SceneMood` component on an entity, from Rendering; the Add menu's Scene Mood), or else a `.fvmood` opened in the asset browser, or else the default mood, unsaved; the viewport draws with that file and the View tab edits it (`MoodDocument`, its edits held by `UnsavedContent` like a material's). It layers them as the game does (`ViewportViewModel.GetLayeredMood`): the lens the main camera names, then the values the mood changes from the defaults, so a canvas or tonemapper picked in the View tab shows over the lens's; the tab's Canvas checkbox (`ShowCanvas`) hides the canvas in the viewport only, and Play keeps it. The mood includes its `Tonemapper`: post-processing ends with a tonemapper picked by enum (`Uncharted2` Hable filmic by default, `Aces`, `None`) and switched in `Post.hlsl`'s `Tonemap`; a new one is an enum value and a case there. Bake Probes (`SceneProbes`) bakes every irradiance volume with that mood on a device of its own into its probes file in the content folder (a volume without one gets `<Scene>.<Volume>.fvprobes` beside the scene), and the viewport's probe cache reloads it. Baking traces rays, so it needs a GPU with ray queries (the status says so otherwise); the viewport itself lights its scene's first volume with live probes while it traces rays, and the file serves stages, previews and GPUs without ray queries. Save (Ctrl+S) writes the world back into the open scene file (`SceneLoader.Save` over `ISceneSpawner.Capture`): one entity per line, components the world left unchanged kept as the file wrote them and changed ones written in full; comments are lost. Save Assets (Ctrl+Shift+S) and Discard Assets save or drop every unsaved material and mood. The asset browser under the viewport (`AssetBrowserViewModel` over `AssetCatalog`) shows the content folder's tree under its search box, a narrow column of type filters and the selected folder's subfolders and files as tiles (`Items`, an icon and colour per type, `AssetTypeLooks`); each type of file is one `AssetType` in `AssetTypes` (its name, extensions, icon, colour and what New writes, `AssetNew`), so a new type of file is one entry there, and the main window opens each type in its editor from one table (`MainWindowViewModel.CreateOpeners`), hidden dot-files skipped; the search looks through everything below the selected folder; it has no buttons: tiles and tree folders have context menus (open, rename, duplicate, delete, new, import, reimport, up, refresh), and a tile dropped on a folder moves there (`MoveTo`); files from outside the editor, dropped on the browser or picked with Import, are copied into the folder they land on or the selected one (`Import`), their sources remembered in the content folder's hidden `.imports` file (`ImportSources`, kept through renames, moves and deletes; the builder skips hidden files and Blender files), so Reimport copies a changed source again; opening a scene replaces the open one (`EditorScene.Open`, `SceneLoader.Unload`), opening a material shows it in the material editor, and a model dragged into the viewport becomes a mesh entity named after it, and a vehicle file a vehicle (`EntityPreset.ForVehicle`), on the mesh or ground under the mouse (`AssetDrag`, `ViewportViewModel.DropAsset`). It creates folders and the files of every type New makes (scenes, vehicles, materials, moods, canvases and flow graphs; a new vehicle is a car on four wheels), named `New Scene` and so on, numbered when taken, naming the content folder's schema, and renames and deletes files and folders, all through `IContentManager` (`Write`, `CreateFolder`, `Move`, `Delete`). A file owns the files in its folder named after it (`AssetCatalog`): a creation (`AssetType.IsCreation`, such as `Van.fvvehicle`) every `Van.<anything>` (its `.blend`, models and paintings), any other file those named after its whole name (an image's `Paper.png.fvtexture` settings), the shortest name winning, so a group's files all start with its name. The browser shows a group as its owner's tile, framed in its type's colour, whose arrow lists the owned files after it (`ToggleExpanded`; `AssetEntry.Owned`, `Owner`, `Label`); renaming, moving and duplicating any of its tiles act on the whole group (Duplicate, Ctrl+D, numbers the copy from 2, a name none of the group's files takes), while Delete takes the whole group from the owner's tile and one file from an owned tile; a type filter lists a matching owned file on its own, `Reveal` expands the group of an owned file, and asset pickers list owned files too (`ContentFileFinder`). With a project open it also shows the project's `Source` folder as a second root, Code (`AssetCatalog.ForCode`, paths under `/Code`, build output skipped, `.cs` files opened in the IDE), where it only writes: New > Flow > Flow Node asks for a name and a code folder (`FlowNodePrompt`: the selected code folder, or the one whose path ends most like the selected content folder's, or the first holding a C# project) and `FlowNodeSource` writes the class there in its project's namespace and adds it to the project's JSON context; it is in flow graphs after the next build. Data assets open in the Data tab (`DataDocument` over `DataTypes.ReadAsset`/`WriteAsset`, edits held by `UnsavedContent`): a form generated from the asset's metadata (`DataObjectViewModel`: a field per value, a section per nested object, and a `DataListViewModel` per list of objects such as fragments, whose Add offers each `$type` registered for the element type); the browser's New Data Asset and New Table submenus list the registered types (`AssetChoice`). Tables open there too (`TableDocument`, `TableViewModel`): a form per row, rows added, renamed and removed, and CSV export and import for spreadsheets (`TableCsv`: a `name` column, then one per property, strings as written and other values as JSON). Every save goes through `IContentManager.Write`, which in a development build also writes the source file the build manifest maps it to, so editor changes outlive the next build.
- Whole trees: duplicating an entity copies everything nested under it (taken names numbered, save ids given free ones, the copies nested under their copied parents), deleting removes the tree deepest first, and the viewport skips selected entities nested under other selected ones. F2 or the outliner's Rename renames an entity (`EditorScene.RenameEntity`, undone as a `RenameStep`), refusing a taken name and rewriting every `[EntityReference]` that names it. Save as Template (the outliner's menu) writes the entity as `Templates/<Name>.fventity` and has it start from it, and the inspector's Template row picks the template an entity starts from (`EditorScene.SetTemplate`, a `TemplateStep`). Nested entities are not part of templates yet.

Project settings (owner, 2026-11-25): the gear in the title bar, before Open in Visual Studio, opens the Project Settings window (`ProjectSettingsWindow` over Core's `ProjectSettingsViewModel`). Its Graphics page lists the edited game's saved rendering console variables (`r.`, Rendering.md), each named for people from its name (`r.RayTracing` is "Ray tracing", `SettingViewModel.Label`) with its help, name and default as the tooltip: a check box for a boolean (`SwitchSettingViewModel`), a list for named values (`ChoiceSettingViewModel`) and a text box otherwise (`TextSettingViewModel`). Setting one sets it in the edited game at once and writes the project's `Settings.fvsettings` through the content (so the source file gets it too, Scenes.md), and Reset to defaults puts every saved variable back and leaves the file holding only its schema. `EditorScene` sets the game's variables from the file whenever it makes a game (opening and Compile) and again when the file changes on disk (`SettingsChanged`, which refreshes the window), so a hand edit while the editor runs takes effect, and a setting that crashes the editor is undone by removing its line before starting it again. The viewport applies the settings every frame (`RenderingSettings.ApplyToViewport`, with `r.ViewportUpscaling` in place of the game window's `r.Upscaling`); its device asks for DLSS and Ray Reconstruction only when the viewport upscales as the editor starts, so switching viewport upscaling on takes effect after a restart.
- Curves (`Core/Curves`, tested headless; step 2 of the sky, light and lens work, #199): `CurveEditorViewModel` overlays number curves (`CurveTrack`: a name, the `Curve` shown, a period it loops over and keeps its keys inside, 24 for a day, and what an edit is committed to) on one `CurveRange` (the times and values the view shows, time across and value upward, panned by pixels, zoomed on either axis or both about a point, framed with room above and below), and the `Active` track takes the pointer: a press takes a free key's slope handle or a key (alone, or with `extend` added to or taken out of the selection; empty space clears it), a drag moves the selected keys (times snapped to `TimeSnap`, a quarter hour, unless `snap` is off) or turns the slope, shown at once and committed once on release, so a drag is one undo step; `AddKey` puts a key on the line at the snapped time (at the pointer on a curve without keys), `DeleteSelected` and `SetMode` change the selected keys at once, a key made free keeping the slopes it had. `GetLine` samples a track across the whole view, a looping one past its period's ends, `GetHandles` places a free key's handles `HandleLength` pixels out, and `Fit` frames every track (a looping one's whole period). A color curve is a `ColorTrack`, edited along its gradient strip in time: one key selected, dragged, added with the color already there, recolored or given a mode. The tracks take a curve their owner changed (after an undo) with `Reset`; undo itself is the owner's file history.
- The curve view (`Shell/Curves`, checked by eye): `CurveView` draws a `CurveEditorViewModel` over a grid of hours (clock labels, the day's ends stronger) and values, every track's line in its own color (`TrackColors`; the active one bright, the rest dim), the active track's keys (selected ones in the accent color) and a free key's slope handles, and a cursor line at `CursorTime`. It hands the editor the pointer: a press takes a key or handle (Shift extends the selection), a drag moves it (Alt without snapping), a double-click adds a key, the middle or right button pans, the wheel zooms (Ctrl the time alone, Shift the value alone), Delete removes the selected keys, F fits, and a right-click without a drag offers the modes. `ColorStrip` draws a `ColorTrack` as a gradient across the same `CurveRange`'s times, its linear colors shown in sRGB, as their fields' color pickers show them, with a marker under each key, and takes presses, drags, double-clicks and Delete the same way.
- Environments in the viewport: `.fvenv` files are the Environment asset type and `.fvlens` files the Lens type (New makes both, an environment with no curves and a lens naming nothing), and the Add menu's Scene Environment places a `sceneEnvironment`. The viewport has an `Hour` (a scene opens at its environment's `startHour`, and one without keeps the hour shown; the scene's own clock is not touched) and builds the mood it draws (`GetDrawnMood`, also what Bake Probes bakes with) as the game does (`GetLayeredMood`): the defaults, the scene's environment at that hour with the sun on the scene's path, the lens its main camera names, then the values the open mood file (`MoodPath`) names, or with no file open the values the View tab's mood changes from the defaults, so a scene's mood lies over its lens; `ShowCanvas` off leaves the canvas out. The View tab writes a mood file sparsely (`MoodDocument`): only the values the file named and the ones edited since, which stay named even when set back, so what lies under the mood shows through the rest.
- The environment editor's model (`Core/Environments`, tested headless): `EnvironmentEditorViewModel` edits a `LightingEnvironment` in place (an `EnvironmentDocument`, `Assets/Environments`, holds it unsaved like a material; `.fvenv` and `.fvlens` edits are kept by `UnsavedContent`). It lists every value an environment can draw (the environment's numbers and colors, not the lens's values or the sun's direction) in its group (Sun, Sky, Fog, Grade) as an `EnvironmentValueViewModel`: the inspector's own field showing the value at the cursor's `Hour` (over the defaults where the environment draws none), whether it is drawn (`IsDrawn`), and whether its curve is shown (`IsSelected`). Editing a field writes a key at the hour rounded to the quarter hour, replacing a key already there but keeping its mode and slopes, and a value without a curve starts one. Selected numbers are tracks of its `Curves` (the last one selected active) and selected colors are `ColorTracks`; an edit there is written back to the environment, and an edit of a field shows on the shown curve. `RemoveCurve` takes a value's curve out, and `Advance` plays the hour on at `Speed` hours a real second while `IsPlaying`, around the day; `HourText` is the hour as a clock shows it.
- The environment editor (`Shell/Environments`, checked by eye): opening an `.fvenv` in the asset browser shows it in an `EnvironmentEditorWindow`, one per file, like a flow graph's, its edits held unsaved and undone through the file's history (Ctrl+Z in the window; after an undo it opens again at the hour it was at). The left side lists the groups' values at the cursor's hour in the inspector's fields, a tick showing a value's curve, its name greyed while the environment draws none, and a right-click offering Remove curve; the right side has the `HourRuler` (the day's hours across the curve view's times, the hours outside the day darker, dragged to move the cursor, snapped to the quarter hour unless Alt is held), a `ColorStrip` for each ticked color, and the `CurveView` of the ticked numbers with the cursor drawn on it; under them the hour as a clock, Play and the hours a real second it plays at. While the window is open the viewport draws its environment in place of the scene's (`ViewportViewModel.EnvironmentPath`) at the cursor's hour, unsaved edits included, and closing it draws the scene's own again.
- Mesh mode (milestone 2 of the creation tools, `Core/Meshes`): in a creator `EnterMesh()` (Tab, wired in the shell by task 2.15) enters the mesh of the selected part, `LeaveMesh()` leaves it; selecting another part leaves it too. The creator is given `findMesh`, which gives a part's solid by id (the shell passes the vehicle panel's). While a mesh is entered a click selects in it and no longer picks parts. `MeshSelection` holds the selected points, edges and faces of the solid as in Blender: `Element` is the kind a click selects (1, 2, 3), the other kinds follow from the selection (a point selection holds the edges and faces whose points are all selected, edges give their points and the faces whose edges are all selected, faces give their edges and points), changing the kind keeps what follows, Shift toggles, a click on nothing clears, `SelectAll` and `Clear`, and `SelectLoop` (Alt) takes the edge loop through an edge, or the strip of faces across the nearest edge of a face (`SolidTopology`, `Scenes.md`). `MeshPicker` finds the element under the mouse on the CPU as `ScenePicker` does: a face by the ray, an edge or a point within 10 pixels on screen, and only edges and points that touch a face turned toward the camera; a solid hiding another part of itself is not tested. A file change that gives the part a new solid keeps the mesh entered and starts the selection again, empty, in the same kind. Drawing the selection comes in the next task, and the tools after it.

The mesh overlay (`MeshOverlay`, drawn by `CreatorMode.DrawMesh` in place of the part's outline while a mesh is entered) shows the edges of the faces turned toward the camera, the points of those faces in point mode, the selected faces filled, the selected edges and points in the selection colour and the element under the mouse (`CreatorMode.Hover`, found like a click).
