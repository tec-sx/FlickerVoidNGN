# Environment

How a place's light changes over the hours of its day (owner, 2026-11-25; issue #199, built task by task).

- Curves (`Curves` in Runtime; for the environment's values over a day, and reusable for anything else drawn over time): a `Curve` is a number over time through `CurveKey`s (`time`, `value`, a `CurveMode` and, for a free key, the slopes `in` and `out` it is arrived at and left at, in value per time), and a `ColorCurve` a color through `ColorKey`s (`time`, `color`, mode), blended channel by channel by the same rules. A key's mode says how the curve travels on to the next key: `Smooth` (the default) eases through the key at the slope between its neighbours, held back (to three times the gentler slope beside it) and flat at a peak or a dip, so the curve never passes above or below its keys; `Linear` is a straight line; `Constant` holds the value until the next key; `Free` uses the key's own slopes (a free color key is smooth). A curve arrives at a smooth key at that key's slope, at a free key at its `in`, and at a linear or constant key straight along the line between the two. Both are immutable and sort their keys by time; of keys sharing a time the later one given is the value there, which makes a step. `Evaluate(time, period)` holds the first and last keys outside them, and with a period (24 for a day; the keys lie from 0 up to it) wraps the time into it, the last key leading on to the first; without keys it gives 0 or black. In files a curve is `{ "keys": [...] }`, read and written by `RuntimeJsonContext`, modes by name and a key's defaults left out.
- Mood values (`Moods` in Rendering): the mood on screen stays one `LightingMood`, and `MoodValues` lists each of its values once, in the mood's own order, as a `MoodValue`: its name as files spell it, the `MoodGroup` it is edited under (`Sun`, `Sky`, `Fog`, `Grade`, `Bloom`, `Tonemapper`, `Canvas`), its `MoodOwner` (`Environment`: the light and air of a place, the grade included, as the owner sets it by hand over the day; `Lens`: bloom, tonemapper, white point and canvas, which the main camera carries) and how to copy and blend it between moods, typed so nothing is boxed: a `MoodNumber` and a `MoodColor` blend evenly, a `MoodDirection` (the sun's) turns rather than shortens, and a `MoodChoice` (tonemapper, canvas, reflection) swaps halfway. A mood's `reflection` (an equirectangular image of all around, such as the engine's CC0 panoramas in `Engine/Rendering/Reflections`) and `reflectionIntensity` are what glossy surfaces mirror (`Shading.hlsl`'s `SampleReflection`, blurrier through the image's mips the less glossy the surface): metal in its own colour, paint and glass by a Fresnel sheen of their highlight's strength and glossiness; without one, metal mirrors the ambient light and paint nothing, as before. `LightingMood.Lerp` blends through the table, and a test fails when a value of the mood is missing from it, so a new value is added in two places: the mood and the table.
- Mood overrides: a `.fvmood` file read as a `MoodOverride` (`RenderingJsonContext.Default.MoodOverride`) is only the values the file names, and `Apply` writes those onto whatever mood is under it and leaves the rest alone, a value named at its default included (`"bloom": 0` turns bloom off over a mood that has it). The same file read as a `LightingMood` is still a whole mood with defaults for the rest, which is how scenes, stages and the director read moods until the layers arrive. An override is checked by the mood's own schema and writes back only what it names.
- Fog range (step 4, #278): the Fog group's `fogRange` (60 units) is how far from the camera along the view the lit fog is lit (Rendering.md); the fog's own density, colour, anisotropy and height values are as before.
- Haze (step 4, #276): the Fog group's `hazeDensity` (0 leaves none), `hazeStart` (2 units behind the view's focus), `hazeMax` (1), `hazeHeight`, `hazeHeightFalloff`, `hazeColor` and `hazeFromSky` (1: the sky's horizon colour; 0: `hazeColor`) are environment values drawn by `Haze.hlsl` (Rendering.md); the far fade's colour melt is gone, replaced by the haze, and the lens's `depthFar*` values now take the colour out of what lies far (L19, #277).
- Environments: a `LightingEnvironment` (a `.fvenv` file, schema `environment`) is a place's light and air over the 24 hours of its day: `numbers` and `colors`, each a curve by the name of the mood value it draws (`{ "numbers": { "exposure": { "keys": [...] } }, "colors": { "fogColor": { "keys": [...] } } }`, key times in hours from 0 up to 24). `Apply(hour, mood)` writes every curve's value at that hour onto the mood and leaves the mood's other values alone, so a value without a curve keeps what lies under it, and one key holds a value all day. Curves wrap around the day (`DayHours`), the last key before midnight leading on to the first after it. Reading a file refuses a curve named for no mood value, for a value of the lens, or of the other kind (a number's curve for a color, or any curve for the sun's direction, which the sky's sun path will turn), so the content check lists a misspelt name.
- The world clock (`WorldClock` in Runtime's `Loop`; `Game.Clock`, also a service): a day and an hour of it (from 0 up to 24, minutes as the fraction, counted in doubles so slow clocks keep time), which pass with the fixed steps at `Speed`, in game hours to a real minute, so it stands still at speed 0 (where every game starts, at hour 0 of day 0) and while the game is paused. `Set(day, hour)` and `Advance(hours)` move it, hours outside the day rolling into the days before or after. The facts follow it: `Time.Hour` (the whole hour, 0 to 23) and `Time.Day` are written when they change, once a whole hour and not every step, and not at all by a clock that never moves, so conditions read the time (`{ "$type": "fact", "fact": "Time.Hour", "compare": "GreaterOrEqual", "value": 20 }`). Writing those facts moves the clock, so fact effects and flows set the time (`Time.Hour` set to 20 is 20:00) or advance it (3 added at 22:30 is 01:00 the next day), and a restored or reset fact database carries it along; while `Time.Held` is above 0 the clock stands still, so a story holds it by adding 1 and lets it go by taking 1 away. Saves keep the facts and, as the `clock` state, the hour to the minute. Nothing expires on the clock: the game stays player-paced, and the clock only moves the light.
- The mood on screen (`MoodDirector`, `Moods` in Rendering) is built from layers, lowest first: the defaults, the environment at the hour of the day, then the overrides of `MoodLayer.Lens` (the lens the main camera names), `Scene` (the mood the scene names), `Story` (the mood the story wants), `Shot` (the shot on screen) and `Debug` (what a tool forces, such as the scene viewer's `--sun`), each changing only the values it names. `SetLayer` and `SetEnvironment` take the seconds the change blends over: the mood on screen blends to the new build, which still follows the hour, and a new change starts from wherever the blend was; 0 shows a change at once, of changes made between two updates the quickest decides (a cut to another scene is not blended by a story mood that changed with it), and nothing blends before the first update. `Current` stays the same object while nothing changes and a mood handed out is never changed afterwards.
- The mood system (`MoodSystem`, a frame system and service added by `game.AddMoods(content)`, which `SceneHost` does: `host.Moods`) feeds the director: the lens the scene's main camera names, the scene's environment (`sceneEnvironment`: `environment`, a `.fvenv`; `speed`, the game hours to a real minute; `startHour`, 12 unless given; and the sun's path, below) and mood (`sceneMood`, or `SceneMoodPath` in its place, which the host's `--mood` sets), followed as the files named change, as on travel, at once; and the story's mood, the mood of the hardest of its `Rules` (`MoodRule`: fact conditions and a mood file) that holds, which blends in and out over `StoryBlendSeconds` (2) as facts change. `SetShotMood(path, seconds)` lays a shot's mood file (null takes it off) and, for a change at once, rebuilds `Current` straight away, so a shot cut to after the frame's update is drawn with its mood on that frame; `ShotMoodSystem` (Cameras) calls it. When a scene with an environment starts playing, the world clock takes its speed, and its start hour when no scene or save has set the time yet (day 1 begins there), so later scenes keep the time and bring their own speed. It builds the mood each frame at the clock's hour. A file edited on disk applies at once; one that is missing or can't be read is logged once and drawn without, or its last version stays. `SceneRenderSystem.MoodSource` hands the mood to the renderer when it is another object than the frame before gave, so the debug panel's tweaks stay until the mood changes. A scene that names only a `sceneMood` looks as it did, as its file's values lie over the defaults; a stage (`Ui`) draws with its scene's mood alone (`MoodOverride.ToMood`).
- The sun's path (`SunPath`, `Sky` in Rendering; step 3, #251): a scene's `sceneEnvironment` also says where the place lies, `latitude` (degrees north, 45 unless given), `north` (which way north lies in the world, degrees turned clockwise seen from above from -Z, so 0 puts east at +X) and `declination` (the sun's, 0 at an equinox, about 23.44 at the northern midsummer; set by hand, as nothing follows the seasons yet), so one environment serves scenes that face different ways. `TowardSun(hour)` is the direction from the ground to the sun, by the hour angle of 15 degrees an hour from noon: east at dawn, south and highest at noon north of the equator, west at dusk, and below the horizon at night. The mood director turns the sun's direction along it (`SetSunPath`, which the mood system takes from the scene as it takes its environment) on top of the environment and under the layers, so a mood file naming `sunDirection` still holds the sun where it says. The sun's light is `sunColor` times `sunIntensity` (environment values of the Sun group, white and 1 unless keyed), which lighting, the fog and the live probes multiply the sun by (the frame data's `SunLight`). The moon (#252) runs the sun's path behind it by its age, the day over `MoonMonthDays` (29.5): beside the sun when new, opposite it when full, rising later each day (`TowardMoon(day, hour)`; the director takes the clock's day, the editor's viewport draws a full moon, `Day`); the path writes `moonDirection` beside `sunDirection`, and `starPole`, the celestial pole the stars turn about (`Pole`: north, as high as the latitude). One light shines from afar and casts the sun's shadows (`DirectionalLight.Of`, which the frame data and the sun's shadow map take): the sun while it is on or above the horizon, else the moon, its `moonColor` times `moonIntensity` times its lit share (`Lit`, from the angle between the two: 0 new, 1 full), fading out as the moon sets too, so a night with neither is lit by the ambient light alone. The swap is not blended: key the sun's and moon's intensities low around sunrise and sunset and the shadows never jump.
- Lenses: a `CameraLens` (a `.fvlens` file, schema `lens`, named by the scene's `mainCamera`) holds the values that belong to the camera rather than the hour, the ones `MoodValues` gives the lens (bloom, the tonemapper and white point, the canvas, the sky's view for orthographic shots `skyFieldOfView`, and the depth tone's `depth...` values), as a mood file spells them and only those it names (`Values`, a `MoodOverride`). A file naming an environment value or no mood value at all can't be read, so the content check reports it, and its schema lists only the lens's values (`ContentSchemas.AddFile` with a schema of its own). The editor's viewport builds its mood as the game does (`ViewportViewModel.GetDrawnMood`, Editor.md), and Play passes that drawn mood when it passes one.
