# Rendering

- Rendering never reads the Arch world while drawing: an `IRenderExtractor` (default `WorldExtractor`) copies it into a plain `RenderScene`, then `SceneRenderer` runs its `RenderPipeline` of `IRenderPass`es (Prepare all, write the frame data, Execute all) over a shared `RenderContext`. New rendering features are new passes added, inserted or replaced in the pipeline, not branches in `SceneRenderer`. After post-processing, `SkyPass` fills what the scene left empty with a sky-like gradient (a thin wash multiplied over a mood's canvas) while the renderer's `Sky` is on: the host's window renderer, the editor's viewport and its camera preview turn it on, while stages, the material preview and probe bakes leave the background empty. The last default pass is `OutlinePass`, like Unreal's custom depth stencil outlines: meshes whose entity has an `outline` component (`Outline`, a color) are drawn as silhouettes in their color into a mask, then a line `Width` pixels wide (2 by default) is drawn around them over the output, seen through whatever stands in front. Indoors (owner, 2026-11-23): an `indoorVolume` (`IndoorVolume`: a box standing on its entity, ambient share, outside muting) scales the mood's ambient light for every pixel inside it (`RenderScene.Indoors`, in the frame buffer after the cluster indices), so closed rooms are dark but for what windows, holes and lamps let in; a `ShadowOnly` entity's meshes go into the shadow atlas but are never seen; and the volume tagged `Cutaway` (`RenderScene.Cutaway`) has every pixel outside its box whose view ray meets the box discarded in the depth prepass and the lit pass (never in shadows), so the room shows through walls, ceilings and ground in front of it, and the world outside it muted, except on `uncut` entities (`Uncut`, such as doors). A mesh's `MeshFlags` (`ShadowOnly`, `OutlineOnly`, `Uncut`) travel with it to its draws and the scene shader's push constants. A `doorway` (`Doorway`: a box standing on its entity, yellow by default) marks a way into or out of a room and is drawn only as its outline, seen through walls; the editor's Greybox group adds one.
- Meshes: a mesh name becomes a model through `MeshSources` (a game service from `AddRendering`, which also sets every renderer the game sets up to use it, `SceneRenderer.Meshes`, else a renderer's own): greybox names first (`GreyboxMesh`), then each `IMeshSource` a module added, in order, then the model the content build baked (`Room.glb` is drawn from `Room.fvmodel`). A source returns a `MadeMesh`, the model and the content file it was made from, or null for a name it doesn't make; solids are drawn through `SolidModel` (one grey part, flat faces, texture coordinates in metres), as greybox boxes are. The renderer's `MeshLibrary` and the editor's `ScenePicker` both ask the same sources and make a mesh again when its file changes, so a creation's parts (such as `Vehicles/Van.fvvehicle#wheel.1L`) are drawn and picked like models. The editor gives its viewport, camera preview and picker the edited game's sources; stages, probe bakes and the material preview keep their renderer's own.
- Culling: a mesh part keeps the box around its vertices from loading (`GpuMeshPart.Bounds`), each draw carries it placed in the world, and the view and every shadow view skip the parts wholly outside their frustum (Runtime's `Geometry/Frustum`, exact, as the GPU would clip them); posed (skinned) parts are never culled, and outlines draw every outlined part. `SceneRenderer.Statistics` (`RenderStatistics`) counts what the last frame drew and culled, shown in the debug Rendering window. `RenderScene.CopyFrom` lets a second renderer draw a scene another extracted.
- Outline requests: several sources may outline an entity at once (`OutlineRequests.Request(world, entity, source, color, priority)`, `Withdraw`, `IsRequested`): its `Outline` is the highest priority's, the latest of equals, or the outline it had before any request (kept in `RequestedOutlines`).
