# Rendering

- Rendering never reads the Arch world while drawing: an `IRenderExtractor` (default `WorldExtractor`) copies it into a plain `RenderScene`, then `SceneRenderer` runs its `RenderPipeline` of `IRenderPass`es (Prepare all, write the frame data, Execute all) over a shared `RenderContext`. New rendering features are new passes added, inserted or replaced in the pipeline, not branches in `SceneRenderer`. After post-processing, `SkyPass` fills what the scene left empty with a sky-like gradient (a thin wash multiplied over a mood's canvas) while the renderer's `Sky` is on: the host's window renderer, the editor's viewport and its camera preview turn it on, while stages, the material preview and probe bakes leave the background empty. The last default pass is `OutlinePass`, like Unreal's custom depth stencil outlines: meshes whose entity has an `outline` component (`Outline`, a color) are drawn as silhouettes in their color into a mask, then a line `Width` pixels wide (2 by default) is drawn around them over the output, seen through whatever stands in front. Indoors (owner, 2026-11-23): an `indoorVolume` (`IndoorVolume`: a box standing on its entity, ambient share, outside muting) scales the mood's ambient light for every pixel inside it (`RenderScene.Indoors`, in the frame buffer after the cluster indices), so closed rooms are dark but for what windows, holes and lamps let in; a `ShadowOnly` entity's meshes go into the shadow atlas but are never seen; and the volume tagged `Cutaway` (`RenderScene.Cutaway`) has every pixel outside its box whose view ray meets the box discarded in the geometry pass (never in shadows), so the room shows through walls, ceilings and ground in front of it, and the world outside it muted, except on `uncut` entities (`Uncut`, such as doors). A mesh's `MeshFlags` (`ShadowOnly`, `OutlineOnly`, `Uncut`, `Greyed`) travel with it to its draws and their records. Two marks let the editor's creator set the rest of a level aside, and neither is a scene component, so no save writes them: `Greyed` draws an entity's meshes black and white (the lighting pass's last step), without their outlines, and leaves its doorways out, so the level shows no markers, and `Undrawn` leaves its meshes and lights out as `Hidden` does, kept apart from `Hidden` so the person's own hiding stays as they left it. A `doorway` (`Doorway`: a box standing on its entity, yellow by default) marks a way into or out of a room and is drawn only as its outline, seen through walls; the editor's Greybox group adds one.
- Meshes: a mesh name becomes a model through `MeshSources` (a game service from `AddRendering`, which also sets every renderer the game sets up to use it, `SceneRenderer.Meshes`, else a renderer's own): greybox names first (`GreyboxMesh`), then each `IMeshSource` a module added, in order, then the model the content build baked (`Room.glb` is drawn from `Room.fvmodel`). A source returns a `MadeMesh`, the model and the content file it was made from, or null for a name it doesn't make; solids are drawn through `SolidModel` (one grey part, flat faces, texture coordinates in metres), as greybox boxes are. The renderer's `MeshLibrary` and the editor's `ScenePicker` both ask the same sources and make a mesh again when its file changes, so a creation's parts (such as `Vehicles/Van.fvvehicle#wheel.1L`) are drawn and picked like models. The editor gives its viewport, camera preview and picker the edited game's sources; stages, probe bakes and the material preview keep their renderer's own.
- Draw records: every draw's data is a record in the frame's buffer (`GpuDraw`, after the indoor volumes): where its mesh stands this frame and the frame before, its vertices then and now, its base color, texture, sampler and material, its outline's color, its `MeshFlags`, and the centre of its box with how wholly it takes that depth (the object depth, below). The scene shader is pushed only the frame buffer and the draw's number, its place among the frame's draws. The renderer remembers each mesh's world matrix from the frame before by its render mesh's id (`PreviousWorlds`; a mesh not drawn in a frame is forgotten, and a new one was where it is), and the frame data carries the view-projection of the frame before; the skinner poses each animated mesh into two sets of buffers in turn, so the pose of the frame before stays readable. Motion vectors and ray tracing read them.
- G-buffer: the geometry pass (`GeometryPass`) draws every mesh the view sees into the scene's depth and the `GBuffer` (`RenderContext.GBuffer`), unlit, cutting away what the cutaway does: the albedo with the paint's coverage in alpha (`Rgba8UNormSrgb`), the world normal folded onto an octahedron (`Rg16Float`), four bytes of material (`Rgba8UNorm`: the material buffer's bindless index in two, how thick the paint stands, 1 where a painting's impasto hides the canvas, and the `MeshFlags`), the object depth (`R16Float`) and the motion (`Rg16Float`: where the surface stood in the frame before less where it stands now, in pixels, from the draw's world and vertices of the frame before and the view of the frame before, as DLSS takes it). The object depth is the view depth the depth tone reads, so small objects darken or fade whole: it leans from the pixel's own depth to its box centre's by the draw's wholeness (`ObjectDepth.Wholeness`: 1 for a box no deeper along the view than `ObjectDepth.WholeSize`, 2 units, falling evenly to 0 at twice as deep; a posed part's box is its rest pose's, placed where its mesh stands). The G-buffer's encodings are in `GBuffer.hlsl`, shared by the passes that write and read it, and the frame data carries the bindless indices of the depth and the G-buffer's targets.
- Lighting: the renderer is deferred. `LightingPass` (compute, `Lighting.hlsl`, a pixel a thread) lights the G-buffer into the HDR target: each surface shaded by its material's controls and lit by the sun, the ambient light, the point and spot lights of its cluster, the shadow atlas, cookies and the probes, seen through the fog, with indoor volumes, the cutaway's muting and `Greyed` applied; alpha is the paint's coverage, more where its impasto is thick, for the canvas, and where the depth is empty the target stays transparent, for the sky or a stage's background. There is no forward pass, as nothing the engine draws blends. Before it, the screen shadows (`ScreenShadowPass`, `ScreenShadows.hlsl`) work out each pixel's ambient occlusion and contact shadow from the depth and the G-buffer's normals, for lighting to read.
- Debug views: `SceneRenderer.DebugView` (`DebugView`: `Lit`, the default, `Albedo`, `Normal`, `Material`, `ObjectDepth`, `Motion` or `Depth`) shows one of the G-buffer's targets or the depth in place of the lit scene (`DebugViewPass`, `DebugView.hlsl`, after the sky and before the outlines, so outlines and the editor's overlays still draw over it): the albedo as painted, the normal as a colour (0.5 + 0.5 * normal), each material in a colour of its own, lighter where its paint stands thick, the object depth and the depth as depth / (depth + 10), and the motion as 0.5 + 0.5 * motion / 8 pixels in red and green; empty pixels are black. The debug Rendering window picks it.
- Culling: a mesh part keeps the box around its vertices from loading (`GpuMeshPart.Bounds`), each draw carries it placed in the world, and the view and every shadow view skip the parts wholly outside their frustum (Runtime's `Geometry/Frustum`, exact, as the GPU would clip them); posed (skinned) parts are never culled, and outlines draw every outlined part. `SceneRenderer.Statistics` (`RenderStatistics`) counts what the last frame drew and culled, shown in the debug Rendering window. `RenderScene.CopyFrom` lets a second renderer draw a scene another extracted.
- Outline requests: several sources may outline an entity at once (`OutlineRequests.Request(world, entity, source, color, priority)`, `Withdraw`, `IsRequested`): its `Outline` is the highest priority's, the latest of equals, or the outline it had before any request (kept in `RequestedOutlines`).
