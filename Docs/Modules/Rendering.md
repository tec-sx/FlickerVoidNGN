# Rendering

- Rendering never reads the Arch world while drawing: an `IRenderExtractor` (default `WorldExtractor`) copies it into a plain `RenderScene`, then `SceneRenderer` runs its `RenderPipeline` of `IRenderPass`es (Prepare all, write the frame data, Execute all) over a shared `RenderContext`. New rendering features are new passes added, inserted or replaced in the pipeline, not branches in `SceneRenderer`. After lighting, `SkyPass` (compute, `Sky.hlsl`) fills what the scene left empty in the HDR target with the mood's sky while the renderer's `Sky` is on: the host's window renderer, the editor's viewport and its camera preview turn it on, while stages, the material preview and probe bakes leave the background empty. Its gradient is the mood's (`skyGround` below the horizon, darkening farther down, `skyHorizon` and `skyZenith` overhead, blended across the horizon over `horizonSoftness`; step 3, #253), by the elevation of each pixel's view ray. An orthographic view, whose rays all point the same way, sees the sky through a virtual lens (#255): the lens's `skyFieldOfView` (60 degrees from top to bottom) looking level along the camera's heading (toward the screen's top when it looks straight down), so its horizon sits midway up the screen however the camera tilts and the sun, moon and stars stand at real directions and move as it turns. Those colours are picked for the screen, so they are turned back through the mood's grade (`Grade.hlsl`: `Ungrade` undoes `Grade`, the exposure, contrast, saturation, tint and tonemapper `Post.hlsl` lays on) and show as picked whatever the grade; it is the thinnest paint (`SkyCoverage`), so over a mood's canvas the canvas's threads show through it whole, and it is painted at the gradient times the canvas's average brightness, a wash as dark as the sky's colours are (a night sky stays dark on bright paper). Over the gradient, as light that the grade and bloom take as they take the scene's, it draws the sun's disc (`sunDiscSize` degrees across, `sunDiscIntensity` times `sunColor`) and its glow (`sunGlow`, fading to nothing `sunGlowSize` degrees out) and the moon's disc (`moonDiscSize`, `moonDiscIntensity` times `moonColor`), shaded as a ball lit from the sun's direction, so it waxes and wanes, with its corona (`moonCorona`, as bright as its lit share), and the stars (#254): drawn by the shader, no texture, one to a cell of a grid round the sky in a frame that turns about `starPole` with the sun, so they wheel through the night with it and stand the same at the same hour each night; each a pixel wide, of its own brightness and a tint between blue and warm white, times `starIntensity`, drowned where the gradient's screen brightness passes 0.02 and fading into the air toward the horizon. The ground hides them all below the horizon. A scene paints its sky with a mask (#256): its `sceneEnvironment`'s `skyMask`, an image imported as a mask (`Mask` in its `.fvtexture`, Content.md), a 4:1 panorama wrapped once round the sky by compass from north (the scene's `north`) at both edges and up from the horizon at the bottom to the zenith at the top (the extractor's `RenderScene.Sky`). Its red takes the plain elevation's place as the position in the gradient (black the horizon's colour, white the zenith's), its green lays the band's `skyBandColor`, its blue holds back the sun's glow and its alpha says where stars show; `skyMaskStrength` blends from the plain gradient (0) to the painted one (1). The brushwork stays put while the colours follow the hour. The pass writes its values each frame to a small upload buffer of its own (`GpuSky`). The sky also takes the haze at its horizon (`SkyHazeAmount`, `Haze.hlsl`: the haze's most at and below the horizon, none from a sine of 0.25 up). Then `DepthFadePass` (`DepthFade.hlsl`) lays the camera's depth tone on the lit scene (Cameras.md: the burn and the far colour loss, by object depth) and the haze (step 4, #276): height fog in closed form, no marching, which melts what lies behind the focus plane by more than `hazeStart` toward its colour by `hazeMax x (1 - exp(-hazeDensity x the distance beyond the start x exp(-height above hazeHeight x hazeHeightFalloff)))` (`HazeAmount`, `Haze.hlsl`), where there is paint, by its coverage; the colour is the sky's horizon colour blended with `hazeColor` by `hazeFromSky`, and is picked for the screen like the sky's, so the pass turns it back through the grade. The frame data carries the haze (560 bytes now, `FrameDataSize`), so everything that reads the depth is done before the image is upscaled and post-processed (`PostProcessPass`: bloom, the grade, the canvas). The last default pass is `OutlinePass`, like Unreal's custom depth stencil outlines: meshes whose entity has an `outline` component (`Outline`, a color) are drawn as silhouettes in their color into a mask, then a line `Width` pixels wide (2 by default) is drawn around them over the output, seen through whatever stands in front. Indoors (owner, 2026-11-23): an `indoorVolume` (`IndoorVolume`: a box standing on its entity, ambient share, outside muting) scales the mood's ambient light for every pixel inside it (`RenderScene.Indoors`, in the frame buffer after the cluster indices), so closed rooms are dark but for what windows, holes and lamps let in; a `ShadowOnly` entity's meshes go into the shadow atlas but are never seen; and the volume tagged `Cutaway` (`RenderScene.Cutaway`) has every pixel outside its box whose view ray meets the box discarded in the geometry pass (never in shadows), so the room shows through walls, ceilings and ground in front of it, and the world outside it muted, except on `uncut` entities (`Uncut`, such as doors). A mesh's `MeshFlags` (`ShadowOnly`, `OutlineOnly`, `Uncut`, `Greyed`) travel with it to its draws and their records. Two marks let the editor's creator set the rest of a level aside, and neither is a scene component, so no save writes them: `Greyed` draws an entity's meshes black and white (the lighting pass's last step), without their outlines, and leaves its doorways out, so the level shows no markers, and `Undrawn` leaves its meshes and lights out as `Hidden` does, kept apart from `Hidden` so the person's own hiding stays as they left it. A `doorway` (`Doorway`: a box standing on its entity, yellow by default) marks a way into or out of a room and is drawn only as its outline, seen through walls; the editor's Greybox group adds one.
- Meshes: a mesh name becomes a model through `MeshSources` (a game service from `AddRendering`, which also sets every renderer the game sets up to use it, `SceneRenderer.Meshes`, else a renderer's own): greybox names first (`GreyboxMesh`), then each `IMeshSource` a module added, in order, then the model the content build baked (`Room.glb` is drawn from `Room.fvmodel`). A source returns a `MadeMesh`, the model and the content file it was made from, or null for a name it doesn't make; solids are drawn through `SolidModel` (one grey part, flat faces, texture coordinates in metres), as greybox boxes are. The renderer's `MeshLibrary` and the editor's `ScenePicker` both ask the same sources and make a mesh again when its file changes, so a creation's parts (such as `Vehicles/Van.fvvehicle#wheel.1L`) are drawn and picked like models. The editor gives its viewport, camera preview and picker the edited game's sources; stages, probe bakes and the material preview keep their renderer's own.
- Draw records: every draw's data is a record in the frame's buffer (`GpuDraw`, after the indoor volumes): where its mesh stands this frame and the frame before, its vertices then and now, its base color, texture, sampler and material, its outline's color, its `MeshFlags`, its part's indices (for rays, which find a hit's triangle there; `Draw.hlsl` reads a record), and the centre of its box with how wholly it takes that depth (the object depth, below). The scene shader is pushed only the frame buffer and the draw's number, its place among the frame's draws. The renderer remembers each mesh's world matrix from the frame before by its render mesh's id (`PreviousWorlds`; a mesh not drawn in a frame is forgotten, and a new one was where it is), and the frame data carries the view-projection of the frame before; the skinner poses each animated mesh into two sets of buffers in turn, so the pose of the frame before stays readable. Motion vectors and ray tracing read them.
- G-buffer: the geometry pass (`GeometryPass`) draws every mesh the view sees into the scene's depth and the `GBuffer` (`RenderContext.GBuffer`), two-sided masters' without culling, back faces turning their normal to the camera, materials without a master through the scene shader (`Scene.hlsl`: `SceneSurface.hlsl` and `Geometry.hlsl`) and then each master's in turn through its own geometry pass (Content.md; `GpuMaterial.Master`, made the first time a material of it is drawn and again when the build changes it), unlit, cutting away what the cutaway does: the albedo with the paint's coverage in alpha (`Rgba8UNormSrgb`), the world normal folded onto an octahedron (`Rg16Float`), four bytes of material (`Rgba8UNorm`: the material buffer's bindless index in two, how thick the paint stands, 1 where a painting's impasto hides the canvas, and the `MeshFlags`), the object depth (`R16Float`) and the motion (`Rg16Float`: where the surface stood in the frame before less where it stands now, in pixels, from the draw's world and vertices of the frame before and the view of the frame before, as DLSS takes it). The object depth is the view depth the depth tone reads, so small objects darken or fade whole: it leans from the pixel's own depth to its box centre's by the draw's wholeness (`ObjectDepth.Wholeness`: 1 for a box no deeper along the view than `ObjectDepth.WholeSize`, 2 units, falling evenly to 0 at twice as deep; a posed part's box is its rest pose's, placed where its mesh stands). The G-buffer's encodings are in `GBuffer.hlsl`, shared by the passes that write and read it, and the frame data carries the bindless indices of the depth and the G-buffer's targets. Cutouts: a material whose `cutoff` (a `Scene.fvshader` parameter, 0 by default) is above 0 is a cutout (`GpuMaterial.Cutout`), such as a card of leaves or a fence: where its paint's coverage falls below the cutoff, the geometry pass and the outline mask leave holes, the shadow atlas draws its parts through `Shadow.hlsl`'s `CutoutMain`, which leaves the same holes, and rays pass there.
- Lighting: the renderer is deferred. `LightingPass` (compute, `Lighting.hlsl`, a pixel a thread) lights the G-buffer into the HDR target: each surface shaded by its material's controls and its master's shading model (`ShadingModel`, a uint and four values after the scene shader's parameters in the material buffer, read by `Material.hlsl`'s `ShadeSurface`, which the probes' rays share: `Painted`; `Metal`, whose highlight takes the albedo's color as it is metal and whose diffuse light fades, as the denoiser's guides follow; `Glow`, whose glow is added and whose scene light is scaled by how lit it is; `Skin`, whose diffuse light wraps further round the terminator in its scatter color by its scatter width, a cheap subsurface scattering, so `Material.hlsl`'s `Diffuse` gives a share of each color of light) and lit by the sun (its direction, and its light, the mood's `sunColor` times `sunIntensity`, Environment.md), the ambient light, the point and spot lights of its cluster, their shadows (traced, below, or from the shadow atlas), cookies and the probes, seen through the fog (step 4, L20, #278: `FogVolumePass`, before it, lights the fog once a frame in a grid of 160 by 90 froxels and 64 slices along the view out to the mood's `fogRange`, logarithmic in a perspective view and even in an orthographic one, with the sun through its shadow map, the cluster lights and the probes (`FogLightMain`, in `Lighting.hlsl`; the shadow atlas only), samples each froxel at a depth in its slice set by interleaved gradient noise across the grid and turned by the golden ratio each frame (`FogJitter`), so neighbouring froxels spread their samples evenly, reads the shadow maps through the same 3x3 filter surfaces use, keeps 90% of the frame before's lighting by reprojecting it by the frame before's view and depth axis (so a still view's fog is steady and its jittered samples smooth), and integrates each column front to back (`FogIntegrateMain`), each froxel blurred with its eight neighbours in its slice by a 3x3 tent (the history keeps them unblurred; fix of 2026-11-27, as random depths and a four-texel shadow lookup left a crawling mottle wherever a lamp lit the fog), into a volume buffer the lighting pass reads with one lookup, `SampleFog`: the light scattered and the share of the surface's light let through; the fog beyond its range is the haze's work; the passes' buffers exist only while the mood has fog, and `FrameData` carries the volume's bindless index, None without one), with indoor volumes, the cutaway's muting and `Greyed` applied; alpha is the paint's coverage, more where its impasto is thick, for the canvas, and where the depth is empty the target stays transparent, for the sky or a stage's background. Its light functions are in `Shading.hlsl`, which the transparent pass shares. Glass (`ShadingModel.Glass`, `GpuMaterial.Transparent`) is the one thing drawn forward: the geometry pass, the shadow maps and the acceleration structures leave it out (light and rays pass through it), and `TransparentPass`, after the sky and before the depth fade, copies the lit scene (`ScreenTargets.Behind`, `FrameData.Behind`), then draws the glass in view far to near through its master's `TransparentMain` (`Transparent.hlsl`), tested against the depth and writing none: what is behind it bent by its normal (`bend`) and blurred (`frost`), times its tint, with a Fresnel reflection of the ambient light and the probes, the sun's (shadowed by the atlas where it is drawn) and the cluster lamps' highlights, and its paint (the surface's alpha) lit as paint; its own light is fogged, and the coverage behind it is kept for the canvas. A pane in front of another sees the scene behind both without the other pane; tinted traced shadows through glass and DLSS's mask over it are to come. Before it, the screen shadows (`ScreenShadowPass`, `ScreenShadows.hlsl`) work out each pixel's ambient occlusion and contact shadow from the depth and the G-buffer's normals, for lighting to read, unless the frame traces rays: then traced occlusion takes their place (below) and traced sun shadows have no need of the contact shadow, so the pass draws nothing. Bindless indices that may differ between neighbouring pixels or rays (a material, a hit draw's buffers and painting, a cookie) are marked `NonUniformResourceIndex` where they index a table (`LoadMaterial`, `RayTracing.hlsl`, `SampleCookie`), as Vulkan asks: unmarked, a GPU may read one thread's for its neighbours.
- Local fog (VE report step 2, 2026-11-27): a `fogVolume` (`FogVolume`: a `box` or `ball` shape filling `size` and standing on its entity as an indoor volume does, its `density`, `color`, `edgeSoftness` (the share of each half size over which it thins to nothing at its edge), `heightFalloff` (thinning upward from its bottom) and `noise`) adds its density to the mood's in the lit fog, its colour weighted by its density, so mist over water, smoke in a room or damp in a cellar is lit by the sun, the lamps and the probes as the rest of the air is (`RenderScene.FogVolumes`; `FogMediumAt` in `Lighting.hlsl`). The froxel grid runs while the mood has fog or the scene has a volume (`FogVolumePass.HasFog`), and the nearest 32 to the focus (else the camera) are drawn. `FogVolumePass` writes them each frame to an upload buffer of its own (`GpuFogHeader`, 32 bytes, then a `GpuFogVolume` of 96 bytes each: its centre, axes and half sizes), whose index it passes in the push constants (`LightingConstants.Fog`), so the frame data keeps its size. The mood's `fogNoise` breaks its own fog up with drifting value noise (two octaves, `fogNoiseScale` units a cell), each volume's `noise` breaks up the volume's, and the noise drifts along `fogWind` at `fogWindSpeed` units a second (Environment.md). A hard edge (`edgeSoftness` 0) that faces the view is grainy on a still frame until the fog's history smooths it, so volumes keep a soft edge unless a wall hides theirs.
- Light beams (VE report step 2, 2026-11-27): while the frame traces rays and the mood's `beamIntensity` is above 0, `LightBeamPass` (after the glass, before the depth fade and the haze, so far beams melt into the air) places beams of sunlight by itself where light breaks through a gap (`LightBeams.hlsl`). `UpdateMain` keeps a beam on each seed of a 16 by 16 grid laid on the world round the focus (else the camera), `beamReach` units each way; a seed's slot follows its cell round the grid, so seeds stand still as the focus moves and keep their beams while in reach. Each finds the ground with a ray straight down from 1.5 units above the focus (so it lands on the floor under a roof or a ceiling, not on top of it), then looks for a gap: a beam stands only where the seed itself sees the sun while most of a ring of 16 points two seeds out round it doesn't (a patch of sun under a window, a broken roof or leaves; fix of 2026-11-27, as the first rule, strongest at half shade, drew a beam along the edge of every open shadow). The share of the ring in shade sets the beam's strength (`smoothstep(0.5, 0.9, share)`, so a straight shadow edge, half shaded, draws none) and the shading blockers' average distance its height, and each beam eases toward both by `1 - exp(-dt / 0.5 s)` a frame (the first frame and a cut set them outright; a new seed starts from nothing). `DrawMain` adds them to the HDR target a pixel a thread, each 8 by 8 tile first gathering the beams that pass near it: a soft column `beamWidth` across from the ground toward the sun, brightest on its axis, fading in over its first tenth and out over its top half, and fading where it passes behind the scene, in the sun's light times `beamColor` times `beamIntensity`. Without ray queries there are no beams.
- Weather (VE report step 6, 2026-11-27): the mood's `rain` and `dust` (0 to 1) drive `WeatherPass` (`Weather`, after the glass), which draws stateless particles from the instance index alone (`Shaders/Weather.hlsl`): rain as thin streaks along the fog's wind and falling (up to 8192 in a 24 by 16 by 24 box anchored to the world around the camera), dust as soft motes drifting with the wind (up to 2048 in a 10 by 5 by 10 box), both additive and depth tested. Rain is lit by `DirectFogLight` (the sun with its shadow and the cluster lights) plus the ambient, dust only by `DirectFogLight`, so motes show where light falls. Rain leaves indoor volumes alone (`IsIndoors` in `Frame.hlsl`): no streaks inside them, and the lighting pass wets only outdoor surfaces (`Wet` in `Lighting.hlsl`: darker albedo, more specular and gloss, most on surfaces facing up). The pass makes its pipeline only once weather is on.
- Debug views: `SceneRenderer.DebugView` (`DebugView`: `Lit`, the default, `Albedo`, `Normal`, `Material`, `ObjectDepth`, `Motion` or `Depth`) shows one of the G-buffer's targets or the depth in place of the lit scene (`DebugViewPass`, `DebugView.hlsl`, after post-processing and before the outlines, so outlines and the editor's overlays still draw over it): the albedo as painted, the normal as a colour (0.5 + 0.5 * normal), each material in a colour of its own, lighter where its paint stands thick, the object depth and the depth as depth / (depth + 10), and the motion as 0.5 + 0.5 * motion / 8 pixels in red and green; empty pixels are black. The debug Rendering window picks it.
- Ray tracing: where the device has ray queries (`IGraphicsDevice.Features` has `GraphicsFeatures.RayQueries`: Vulkan's acceleration structures and ray queries, as RTX cards and lavapipe have; `GraphicsDeviceOptions.Features` can turn them off), `SceneRenderer.RayTracing` turns the ray traced tier on (`RenderContext.RayTracing`; off by default, and always off without ray queries): the host's window renderer, the editor's viewport and probe bakes turn it on, and a transition's dissolve follows the frame it draws in, while stages and previews leave it off. `RayTracingPass`, after skinning and the shadows and before the geometry pass, then builds the scene's acceleration structures every frame: a bottom-level structure for each mesh part as loaded, built once and shared by every mesh drawing it, and one for each posed part of each animated mesh, refitted to this frame's pose (`MeshStructures`; those no draw used in a frame are released), then the top-level one placing every draw but outline-only ones, in the view or not, opaque and seen from both sides, with its draw's number as its instance id, so a hit's `CommittedInstanceID()` names its draw record. The frame data names it (`FrameData.AccelerationStructure`, a slot of the bindless acceleration structures, `RaytracingAccelerationStructure AccelerationStructures[]` at `t0, space5`, a set only devices with ray queries have); slot 0 holds an empty structure every ray misses. Graphics builds them through `IGraphicsDevice.CreateBottomLevel` and `CreateTopLevel` and `ICommandList.BuildBottomLevels` (batched, sharing the command list's scratch, whose smaller buffer a larger one replaces only once the builds recorded with it have run) and `BuildTopLevel` (`AccelerationStructureInstance`s in a buffer made with `BufferUsage.AccelerationStructureInput`); each build waits for what came before it and is readable by whatever comes after. Cutout draws are placed not opaque, so a ray query hands their hits to its shader, which commits them only where the paint covers the triangle (`RayTracing.hlsl`'s `TraceVisibility`, reading the triangle's corners through the draw record's indices and vertices). Ray traced shadows (owner, 2026-11-25): while the frame traces rays, `ShadowRayPass`, after the geometry pass, traces from every pixel the sun's shadow and those of the first three shadow-casting lamps in scene order (`ShadowRayPass.LampSlots`; `LightGatherPass` gives each a channel, `GpuLight.RayShadow`, and the rest shadow maps) into one texture, the sun's in red (`FrameData.RayShadows`, which the lighting pass reads in place of the shadow maps), so traced shadows reach as far as the scene does. Each pixel traces a ray a light a frame, at a point on the light: within the sun's width (`LightingMood.SunAngle`, in degrees across, 1 by default) of its direction, or on a disk of the lamp's `SourceRadius` (`PointLight` and `SpotLight`, 0.05 by default) facing the pixel, so shadows are sharp beside their casters and soften away from them, and 0 keeps them sharp. The points follow the R2 low-discrepancy sequence, turned for each pixel and light by a hash; a filter (`ShadowRays.hlsl`'s `FilterMain`) averages each pixel's samples over up to 16 frames where the history lands on the same surface (by the G-buffer's motion and the view depth), clamping the history to the spread of this frame's samples round the pixel and its own, so a shadow follows its caster at once. While Ray Reconstruction denoises the frame (below), the points are white noise instead and the filter is left out. Shadow maps then serve only the fog, which marches through air rays don't light: the sun's and the traced lamps' are drawn only while the mood has fog. Ray traced ambient occlusion: the same pass traces a ray a pixel a frame over the surface's hemisphere, more often near its normal, as long as the mood's `AmbientOcclusionRadius`, and counts what it meets, so corners the view sees edge on and things it leaves out (off screen, or nearer than the near plane) darken the ambient and bounced light as the mood's `AmbientOcclusion` says. Its average over the frames (`FrameData.RayOcclusion`, the red of the filter's history, beside how many frames it holds and the surface's view depth) isn't clamped to the samples round the pixel, as the few rays that meet something in an open corner would be clamped away, so it lags a moving occluder by a few frames.
- Global illumination (owner, 2026-11-25): an `irradianceVolume` (`IrradianceVolume`: a box round its entity filled with a grid of probes, each in the middle of its cell) lights what stands in it, and on its sides, with bounced light. Each probe holds its light as first-order spherical harmonics already convolved for diffuse surfaces (`IrradianceProbe`) and an octahedral map of how far it sees, folded as the G-buffer folds normals (16 by 16 texels of the mean and mean squared distance of their directions, as shares of the probe's reach, 1.5 times a cell's diagonal; `IrradianceProbes.Distances`). `Probes.hlsl`'s `SampleProbes` blends the eight probes round a point, each weighed by how it faces the surface and by Chebyshev's bound on whether it sees the point, judged a little off the surface toward the camera, so light doesn't pass through walls or closed doors (dynamic diffuse global illumination, Majercik et al. 2019). `ProbePass`, after `RayTracingPass`, finds the frame's probes (`RenderContext.ProbeSource`): while the frame traces rays and the renderer's `LiveProbes` is on (the default; the debug interface's Rendering panel switches it), it traces the scene's first volume live. Every frame each probe sends `ProbePass.LiveRays` (128) rays spread evenly over the sphere (a spherical Fibonacci set) and turned anew, finds the light each meets (`ProbeRays.hlsl`'s `TraceMain`: the surface lit as the lighting pass lights it by the sun and the lamps, their shadows traced, the ambient light and the probes themselves, so light bounces on from frame to frame, plus its glow, but for the painted touches that depend on the view; nothing from a surface's back or where it meets nothing, as the ambient light stands for the sky), and `UpdateMain` folds them into its light and its distances, keeping 97% of what they held; the first frame, and any after the volume moves or changes, keeps nothing. Otherwise a frame lights with another renderer's probes (`SceneRenderer.ProbesFrom`: a transition's dissolve shares its owner's) or with those baked into the volume's file (`ProbeCache`). `IrradianceBaker` bakes a volume through the same pass (`ProbePass.Bake`): a frame of its own renderer a bounce (3 by default), `ProbePass.BakeRays` (1024) rays a probe, each frame keeping nothing of the one before but the light its rays meet, which the probes of the bounce before light; it needs ray queries and throws `GraphicsUnavailableException` without. A `.fvprobes` file (version 2) holds the probe counts, then every probe's light, then every probe's distances (`IrradianceProbes.WriteTo`, the layout the GPU reads); a version 1 file, which has no distances, asks to be baked again.
- Upscaling: where the device has DLSS (`GraphicsFeatures.Dlss`: an RTX GPU with the engine's DLSS library beside the program; `IGraphicsDevice.CreateUpscaler` makes an `IUpscaler` for an `UpscalerDescription`, which picks the render size), `SceneRenderer.Upscaling` (`UpscaleMode`: `Off`, the default, `Quality` or `Dlaa`; ignored without DLSS, so every other GPU draws as before) draws the scene at the upscaler's render size (`RenderContext.RenderWidth` and `RenderHeight`, the frame data's `TargetSize`, the scene's targets; `Width`, `Height` and `View` stay the output's, for the passes after the upscale), its view moved each frame by the Halton (2, 3) jitter after the orthographic pixel snap (`JitterSequence`: eight phases for every output pixel a render pixel covers, 18 for Quality, and at least 32 while Ray Reconstruction denoises; `CameraView.Jittered`, `RenderContext.Jitter`, the frame data's `Jitter` and `PreviousJitter`), and its textures sharpened by a mip bias of log2(render width over output width) − 1 (`SamplerDescription.MipBias`). The G-buffer's motion and the frame data's view of the frame before are without the jitter, the traced shadows' history steps by both frames' jitter, and outlines draw at the output's size without it (`Scene.hlsl`'s `SilhouetteVertexMain`). `UpscalePass`, after the depth fade, gives the upscaler the lit scene, its depth and motion (`UpscaleInputs`, with the mood's exposure), which fills the output-size HDR target (`ScreenTargets.Upscaled`, which can also be cleared, as DLSS clears it), then carries the scene's alpha over (`Upscale.hlsl`: the paint's coverage and thickness, filtered where the jitter moved each surface), as DLSS leaves it out; bloom and post-processing read it at the output's size. A frame starts what passes keep from frame to frame afresh (`RenderContext.ResetsHistory`: the upscaler's history, the traced shadows') on the first frame, when the size or the upscaling changes, and when the director cuts to a new view (`ActiveCamera.Cuts`, `RenderScene.Cuts`). The debug view reads the G-buffer at the render size across the whole output, the editor's grid tests the scene's depth itself, read at the render size, and `RenderStatistics` has the render and output sizes and whether the frame was upscaled and denoised, which the debug Rendering window shows beside its upscaling switch (Off, DLSS Quality or DLAA) and its Ray Reconstruction switch.
- Bloom (`PostProcessor`, `Bloom.hlsl`, after Froyok's): the lit scene goes through a 13-tap downsample chain whose level count follows the output's height (8 from half size at 1080p, `ScreenTargets.BloomLevelsFor`), and each level on the way up blends the one below into itself, `lerp(current, below, bloomRadius)`: 0 keeps the tight glow, 1 the widest (0.85 by default). `bloom` (the lens's amount, at most about 0.3) lerps the scene toward the result in `Post.hlsl`, by `saturate(bloom + dirt * dirtAmount)`, where `dirt` is the lens's grey dirt picture (`Dirt`, none is a clean lens; the bloom runs when a dirt is set), so dirt shows only where there is light. The vignette (`vignetteIntensity`, `vignetteRadius` and `vignetteSoftness` as shares of the distance to the corner, `vignetteRoundness` 0 following the screen and 1 a circle, `vignetteColor`) is laid in `Post.hlsl` after the tonemapper and the grade, on painted pixels only, so the canvas's bare paper keeps its edge. Post's push constants keep `float3`s on 16-byte rows (a pad before `VignetteColor`), as HLSL packs them. Lens flares (`LensFlares`, `Flare.hlsl`, after Froyok's) read the half-size bloom level before the upsample: `ThresholdMain` takes it to a quarter of the output through the 13-tap filter with a soft threshold (`flareThreshold`, rising over `flareThresholdRange`), `BlurMain` blurs it once (Dual Kawase), and `GhostsMain` copies it into `ScreenTargets.Flare` as the ghosts of the lens's `.fvflare` file (`FlareFile`: up to eight `FlareGhost`s, each a scale about the middle, negative mirrored, and a colour; `chromaShift` splits red and blue along the line from the middle; `edgeFade`), each faded at its rim and the screen's edge, and over them John Chapman's halo (`haloRadius` and `haloWidth` in screen heights, `haloIntensity`, `haloChroma` parting red and blue rings), which rings the middle with the light lying near it; `FinishMain` blurs both once more and tints them from `centerColor` in the middle to `edgeColor` at the edge, times `flareIntensity`. The passes write `FlarePing` and `FlarePong` in turn and finish in `Flare`. The glare (`Glare.hlsl`, after Arkham Knight by way of Froyok; on when the flare file's `glareIntensity` is above 0): `FindMain` takes one point per 2x2 block of the threshold, five taps, and appends those brighter than `glareThreshold` to a list of at most 1024 (its count copied to zero first), then one instanced draw of 1024 stars of three quads, 60 degrees apart from `glareAngle`, `glareLength` long and `glareWidth` wide in screen heights, adds them to `Flare` (soft across, fading along); instances past the count collapse, so no indirect draw is needed. `Post.hlsl` adds the flare to the image, raised by the lens's dirt (`flare * (1 + dirt * dirtAmount)`), so it shows with no bloom.
- DLSS: a device turns it on only when asked (`GraphicsDeviceOptions.Features` with `GraphicsFeatures.Dlss`, which `SceneHost` asks for and draws with in `Quality`; the editor's viewport draws without it until its settings switch it on) and only where NGX runs: the engine's DLSS library (`Source/Native/Dlss`, a C API in `FlickerVoid.Dlss.h` over the static NGX library of NVIDIA's DLSS SDK) must be beside the program, NGX must name the instance and device extensions it needs, which the device enables (and buffer device addresses with them; `VulkanDlss` asks for the device extensions once a process for each GPU model and driver, as NGX stops naming them once it has shut down on a device), and DLSS must start on the GPU; otherwise the device logs why and runs without it. `build.ps1` (Windows: git, CMake and Visual Studio's C++ tools) and `build.sh` (Linux: git, CMake and a C++ compiler) fetch the SDK (v310.9.16: only its headers, static library and runtimes) and the Vulkan headers into `artifacts/native`, build the library and put it with NVIDIA's DLSS and Ray Reconstruction runtimes (`nvngx_dlss.dll` and `nvngx_dlssd.dll`, `libnvidia-ngx-dlss.so.310.9.16` and `libnvidia-ngx-dlssd.so.310.9.16`; `-Development` or `--development` takes the ones with NVIDIA's indicator, debug keys and watermark) in `artifacts/native/win-x64` or `linux-x64`, which `Graphics.Vulkan` copies beside every program that references it; nothing of the SDK is in the repository. NGX knows the engine by a project id (a GUID in `VulkanDlss`) and writes its log under the local application data's `FlickerVoid/Dlss`, and the library serializes its calls, as NGX is not thread safe. `VulkanUpscaler` draws at NGX's optimal size (Quality two thirds of the output, DLAA the output's), makes its DLSS feature (HDR, motion at the render size without the jitter) in the command buffer of the first frame it upscales, clears the mood's exposure into a 1×1 `R16Float` image each frame for DLSS to read, evaluates in the frame's command buffer and binds the bindless tables again after, as NGX leaves its own bound; its feature is released once the GPU is done with it, and NGX stops before the device is destroyed. A game that ships DLSS shows NVIDIA's marks on its splash, about box and credits (`THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md`).
- Ray Reconstruction: where the device has it too (`GraphicsFeatures.RayReconstruction`, asked for beside `Dlss`, as `SceneHost` does: NVIDIA's Ray Reconstruction runtime must be beside the program and NGX must run it on the GPU; `VulkanDlss` enables its instance and device extensions with DLSS's, asks NGX about its device extensions on every device that asks for DLSS, so a later device in the process still finds them, and otherwise logs why it is off), a frame that traces rays and upscales denoises as it upscales (`SceneRenderer.RayReconstruction`, on by default; `UpscalerDescription.Denoises`, `RenderContext.Denoises`): DLSS Ray Reconstruction takes the noise of the traced shadows and occlusion out with the jitter's in one pass, in place of their filter and plain DLSS. The shadow pass then leaves its filter out and the lighting pass reads its raw samples, and the samples are white noise drawn afresh each frame (a hash of the pixel, the light and `FrameData.NoiseFrame`, which is None otherwise), as Ray Reconstruction asks for samples without pattern; the fog's jitter keeps its pattern, as the fog is lit and blurred before Ray Reconstruction sees it. `UpscalePass` first draws the denoiser's guides at the render size from the G-buffer (`DenoiseGuides.hlsl`; `UpscaleInputs.Guides`, a `DenoiseGuides`): the diffuse albedo, linear and muted and greyed as the lit surface is (middle grey where nothing was drawn), the specular albedo, the material's highlight strength through NVIDIA's fit for the share a rough surface reflects toward the view (EnvBRDFApprox2; black where nothing was drawn), and the world normal with the roughness in alpha, the square root of the microfacet alpha of the Blinn-Phong highlight the glossiness draws (zeros where nothing was drawn), with the view without the jitter. `VulkanUpscaler` draws at Ray Reconstruction's optimal size, makes its feature (the depth buffer's own depth, the roughness packed in the normals' alpha; its transformer model, preset E, as the SDK's default model, preset F, speckled round the walking fox and smeared the edges of the boxes in front of it on the owner's RTX 5060 Ti, where E and D didn't) and gives it no exposure, which it doesn't take. The debug Rendering window switches it and says when a frame was denoised. Whether Ray Reconstruction takes the orthographic view as well as the perspective one is checked on an RTX GPU, as CI's GPUs have no DLSS.
- Culling: a mesh part keeps the box around its vertices from loading (`GpuMeshPart.Bounds`), each draw carries it placed in the world, and the view and every shadow view skip the parts wholly outside their frustum (Runtime's `Geometry/Frustum`, exact, as the GPU would clip them); posed (skinned) parts are never culled, and outlines draw every outlined part. `SceneRenderer.Statistics` (`RenderStatistics`) counts what the last frame drew and culled, shown in the debug Rendering window. `RenderScene.CopyFrom` lets a second renderer draw a scene another extracted.
- Instance batches (VE report step 3, drawing in bulk): copies of one model drawn together, such as a chunk of grass or the bushes along a street, without an entity a copy. A chunk entity carries an `InstancedMesh` (`Instancing/`, runtime-only, so scenes never save it) holding an `InstanceBatch`: the model and its materials, the copies, the scale range (`MinScale`, `MaxScale`), an optional `StandIn` model the whole batch draws beyond `StandInDistance` from the focus (else the camera), and a `Version`. Whatever owns the chunk (a scatter, a brush, the far view) writes the copies with `Rewrite(count)` or changes them in place and calls `Changed()`; the renderer only reads them. A copy (`Instance`, 16 bytes, `Shaders/Instance.hlsl`) is its world position and one word packing its turn about the vertical (256 steps), its size as a share of the range, its variant and its wind phase; copies stand upright. The extractor copies batches into `RenderScene.InstanceBatches` (not hidden or undrawn ones). `InstancingPass`, after skinning, keeps each batch's copies in an upload buffer per frame in flight, rewritten when that frame's copy is older than the batch's version (so a brush can rewrite a chunk every frame without allocating), works out the box round the copies when they change, culls the batch as a whole against the view and adds a draw per part of its model at the identity (`MeshDraw.Instanced`, the scale range in the draw's record). For the batches in view it then culls the copies one by one in compute (`Instancing.hlsl`: each copy's sphere, the model's sized as the copy, against the view's planes), writes the visible list and each part's indirect arguments, which the geometry pass draws with (`ICommandList.DrawIndexedIndirect`, `BufferUsage.Indirect`); the vertex stage places a copy by its instance's number through the visible list, and the depth tone takes the copy's own centre. Shadow views cull a batch as a whole and draw all its copies; the ray tracing scene places a copy an instance, the batches nearest the focus first, up to `RayTracingPass.MaxTracedCopies`, and hits turn their normal by the instance's transform. Outlines and glass leave batches out. `RenderStatistics` counts the batches drawn and culled, the copies sent to the cull and the copies the GPU kept (read back from the frame slot's last frame, so three frames late), shown in the debug Rendering window. Wind and variants are carried but not used yet.
- Profiling (VE report step 1, 2026-11-27): a command list times its own GPU work (`ICommandList.BeginTimer(name)` and `EndTimer`, nested, at most `GraphicsLimits.TimersPerCommandList` a recording, the rest dropped; `End` throws while one is open). `VulkanCommandList` writes a timestamp query at each end once the GPU has finished everything recorded before it, and reads them on its next `Begin`, which has already waited for that submission, so reading never stalls; `Timings` then holds the last submission's `GpuTiming`s (name, depth, start and length in milliseconds), empty before the first submission and where the queue writes no timestamps (`GraphicsFeatures.Timestamps`, found, not asked for: the queue family's valid timestamp bits and the device's timestamp period). While `SceneRenderer.Profiling` is on (off by default; the debug interface's Profiler turns it on once shown), `RenderPipeline` times each pass's `Prepare` and `Execute` on the CPU and wraps its `Execute` in a GPU timer named after it (`IRenderPass.Name`, its type's name by default), the renderer times the extraction (`Extract`) and the frame data's write (`FrameData`), and takes the command list's timings at the start of each frame; the host times its overlay (`Overlay`). `RenderProfile` adds each name's times per frame, averages them over a window of frames (`DefaultWindow`, 60, about a second) and publishes them when the window is over, in the order they first ran (`Passes`, `PassTiming`), with the frame's totals (`CpuMilliseconds`; `GpuMilliseconds`, the outermost timers only). The GPU times come from the frame the command list last drew, three frames back in the host. The renderer reads every timer on its command list, so only one renderer should profile on a list. The debug Profiler window draws the outermost passes as boxes side by side on a CPU row and a GPU row against a line at 16.7 ms, a frame at 60 frames per second (the timeline is at least a quarter wider than that), with each pass's name and time on hover, and a table of every pass's CPU and GPU time, nested GPU timers indented without a CPU time. A debug window opens at its `DefaultSize` the first time it is shown (zero fits its contents), before the saved layout remembers it.
- Outline requests: several sources may outline an entity at once (`OutlineRequests.Request(world, entity, source, color, priority)`, `Withdraw`, `IsRequested`): its `Outline` is the highest priority's, the latest of equals, or the outline it had before any request (kept in `RequestedOutlines`).
- Rendering settings (owner, 2026-11-25): the renderer's feature switches are console variables (`RenderingSettings`, a game service `AddRendering` adds, its variables in `Game.DebugConsole`): `r.LocalLights`, `r.Probes`, `r.RayTracing`, `r.LiveProbes`, `r.Upscaling` (the game window's, `Quality` by default), `r.ViewportUpscaling` (the editor viewport's, `Off` by default, read as the editor starts, as its device asks for DLSS only then), `r.RayReconstruction`, `r.DepthFades` and `r.DebugView` (not saved). `Apply` sets a game window's renderer as they say and `ApplyToViewport` the viewport's, with its own upscaling; `Changed` is raised when any is set. `SceneHost` reads the content's `Settings.fvsettings` (Scenes.md) as it starts, logging what it skipped, and applies the variables to its renderer whenever they change, so `r.RayTracing 0` in the debug Console turns ray tracing off at once; the debug Rendering window switches through the game's settings when it is given them (`AddRenderingWindows`' `settings`), so it and the console agree, or through its own captured from the renderer (`RenderingSettings.Capture`). `r.VSync` (owner, 2026-11-27; off by default) sets the game window's swapchain present mode: FIFO with it on, otherwise mailbox, else immediate, so the frame rate is not capped at the refresh rate; the window loop never sleeps.
