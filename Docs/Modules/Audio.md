# Audio

Design: the "Audio system base: design" doc (owner, 2026-11-27). This is step 1, sound out; the music and ambience layers (step 2), Steam Audio's 3D sound (step 3) and room reverb (step 4) follow.

- The engine mixes sound itself (owner, 2026-11-27; FMOD stays possible later): the `Audio` module (over Flow and Rendering; `game.AddAudio()`) has a mixer of its own, so every voice can start on an exact sample, which the bar-synced music of step 2 needs. Gameplay only knows `IAudioEngine` (play a `sound` data asset as it is, at a place or on an entity; stop; is it playing), never the mixer, so another backend can stand behind it.
- The mixer (`Mixing`: `Mixer`) works in one format, interleaved stereo floats at 48 kHz in blocks of at most 256 frames (`AudioFormat`). The game thread sends `MixerCommand`s (play a source on a bus with a gain to each ear and a low-pass cutoff, on a mixer frame; set a voice; stop it over a fade; set or pause a bus) over a lock-free queue, and the audio thread takes them up before each block, mixes up to 64 voices into the buses (`AudioBus`: `Music`, `Ambience`, `Effects`, `Dialogue`, `Interface`) and the buses into `Master`, each with a gain ramped across the block (so no change clicks) and a one-pole low-pass (`LowPass`, its cutoff followed over a few blocks), clamps the output to full scale and counts the frames rendered (`Position`, the clock everything is scheduled on). `Render` neither allocates nor locks. Voices that end (their source ran out, they were stopped, or no voice was free) come back by id through `TryTakeFinished`. A voice scheduled on a frame starts on that frame, mid-block when need be, so two voices scheduled together stay in step. A paused bus holds its voices where they are.
- A voice plays an `ISoundSource` (fill a block, say how many frames were written); `ClipSource` plays a decoded `SoundClip` (mono or stereo at the file's rate) at a pitch, once or looping, resampled by linear interpolation. Streams and instruments are later sources.
- Devices (`Devices`): `SdlAudioDevice` plays a mixer through SDL2's default output (`Silk.NET.SDL`, which the window already ships): SDL's audio thread pulls blocks from `Mixer.Render`. `TryOpen` gives null with the reason where no device opens, and takes an SDL driver name (`dummy` plays to nowhere, which its test uses). `NullAudioDevice` renders the mixer into memory when asked, so tests hear exactly what a device would play, to the sample. `SceneHost` opens the SDL device when it starts, if the game added audio, and logs once and stays silent without one.
- Sounds (`Sounds`): a `sound` data asset (`SoundDefinition`) names its `files` (`.wav` or `.ogg`, variations picked at random and never the same twice running), `gain` and `pitch` with a variation each, its `bus`, whether it is `spatial` (with `minDistance`, full gain within it, and `maxDistance`, gone at it), `loop` and `throttle` (the shortest time between two plays; a sooner play is dropped). `SoundLibrary` decodes files whole when first played (`WavReader`: PCM of 8, 16, 24 and 32 bits and 32-bit floats; `OggReader`: Vorbis through NVorbis) and forgets one when its file changes on disk, so an edited sound is heard on its next play. The builder copies sound files as they are. Long files stream from step 2.
- `AudioSystem` (a frame system, the `IAudioEngine` service and its own) plays sounds as voices and keeps the spatial ones placed each frame: gain falling with distance and an equal-power pan by the angle to the listener's right (`Panning`; never fully in one ear), following the entity a sound plays on. The listener is the first `audioListener` entity (`AudioListener`: the ears' `height` above its place), its ears turned with the camera the frame is drawn from (`ActiveCamera.Find`), so the left of the screen sounds from the left; without a listener the camera hears. A `soundEmitter` (`SoundEmitter`: `sound`) plays its sound on its entity from activate to deactivate, such as a lamp's hum or, with a sound that is not spatial, a scene's ambience bed. `PlaySoundCommand` (an event: the sound, a position or an entity) plays one from any system; the `playSound` effect and flow node play one where the context's target stands, or as it is without a target. A sound that can't be played (no such asset, a missing or broken file) is logged once and gives `SoundHandle.None`.
- While the listener stands in an indoor volume (Rendering's `IndoorVolume`), the `Ambience` bus is lowered and low-passed (`IndoorAmbienceGain` 0.6, `IndoorAmbienceCutoff` 1200 Hz), so the street is dull inside; Steam Audio's occlusion replaces this in step 3. While the game is paused, the `Ambience`, `Effects` and `Dialogue` buses are held, and `Music` and `Interface` play on.
- The volumes are console variables (`AudioSettings`): `audio.Master`, `audio.Music`, `audio.Ambience`, `audio.Effects`, `audio.Dialogue` and `audio.Interface`, from 0 to 1, which the settings file sets.
- Saves keep nothing of sound: what plays is rebuilt from the scene's emitters.
