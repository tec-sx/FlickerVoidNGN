# Audio

Design: the "Audio system base: design" doc (owner, 2026-11-27). Steps 1 (sound out) and 2 (the music and ambience layers) are built; a time stretch that keeps pitch, Steam Audio's 3D sound (step 3) and room reverb (step 4) follow. The music editor the owner approved as mockups (2026-11-27: an arrangement of lanes with rehearsal rows, a mixer, a live dock in the level) comes after them and edits the `music` data assets described here.

- The engine mixes sound itself (owner, 2026-11-27; FMOD stays possible later): the `Audio` module (over Flow, Rendering and Cameras; `game.AddAudio()`) has a mixer of its own, so every voice can start on an exact sample, which the bar-synced music of step 2 needs. Gameplay only knows `IAudioEngine` (play a `sound` data asset as it is, at a place or on an entity; stop; is it playing), never the mixer, so another backend can stand behind it.
- The mixer (`Mixing`: `Mixer`) works in one format, interleaved stereo floats at 48 kHz in blocks of at most 256 frames (`AudioFormat`). The game thread sends `MixerCommand`s (play a source on a bus with a gain to each ear and a low-pass cutoff, on a mixer frame; set a voice; stop it over a fade, at once or from a mixer frame; set or pause a bus; `PlayOnGrid` also fades a voice in and, when its frame has passed by the time the command arrives, starts it from where it would have been, so nothing scheduled late falls off the grid) over a lock-free queue, and the audio thread takes them up before each block, mixes up to 64 voices into the buses (`AudioBus`: `Music`, `Ambience`, `Effects`, `Dialogue`, `Interface`) and the buses into `Master`, each with a gain ramped across the block (so no change clicks) and a one-pole low-pass (`LowPass`, its cutoff followed over a few blocks), clamps the output to full scale and counts the frames rendered (`Position`, the clock everything is scheduled on). `Render` neither allocates nor locks. Voices that end (their source ran out, they were stopped, or no voice was free) come back by id through `TryTakeFinished`. A voice scheduled on a frame starts on that frame, mid-block when need be, so two voices scheduled together stay in step. A paused bus holds its voices where they are.
- A voice plays an `ISoundSource` (fill a block, say how many frames were written); `ClipSource` plays a decoded `SoundClip` (mono or stereo at the file's rate) at a pitch, once or looping, resampled by linear interpolation. `GridLoopSource` loops a clip on a period of mixer frames whatever the clip's own length (a longer one is cut, a shorter one followed by silence), from an offset into the period, which is how music loops stay in step. Streams and instruments are later sources.
- Devices (`Devices`): `SdlAudioDevice` plays a mixer through SDL2's default output (`Silk.NET.SDL`, which the window already ships): SDL's audio thread pulls blocks from `Mixer.Render`. `TryOpen` gives null with the reason where no device opens, and takes an SDL driver name (`dummy` plays to nowhere, which its test uses). `NullAudioDevice` renders the mixer into memory when asked, so tests hear exactly what a device would play, to the sample. `SceneHost` opens the SDL device when it starts, if the game added audio, and logs once and stays silent without one.
- Sounds (`Sounds`): a `sound` data asset (`SoundDefinition`) names its `files` (`.wav` or `.ogg`, variations picked at random and never the same twice running), `gain` and `pitch` with a variation each, its `bus`, whether it is `spatial` (with `minDistance`, full gain within it, and `maxDistance`, gone at it), `loop` and `throttle` (the shortest time between two plays; a sooner play is dropped). `SoundLibrary` decodes files whole when first played (`WavReader`: PCM of 8, 16, 24 and 32 bits and 32-bit floats; `OggReader`: Vorbis through NVorbis) and forgets one when its file changes on disk, so an edited sound is heard on its next play. The builder copies sound files as they are. Files still decode whole; long songs will want streaming.
- `AudioSystem` (a frame system, the `IAudioEngine` service and its own) plays sounds as voices and keeps the spatial ones placed each frame: gain falling with distance and an equal-power pan by the angle to the listener's right (`Panning`; never fully in one ear), following the entity a sound plays on. The listener is the first `audioListener` entity (`AudioListener`: the ears' `height` above its place), its ears turned with the camera the frame is drawn from (`ActiveCamera.Find`), so the left of the screen sounds from the left; without a listener the camera hears. A `soundEmitter` (`SoundEmitter`: `sound`) plays its sound on its entity from activate to deactivate, such as a lamp's hum or, with a sound that is not spatial, a scene's ambience bed. `PlaySoundCommand` (an event: the sound, a position or an entity) plays one from any system; the `playSound` effect and flow node play one where the context's target stands, or as it is without a target. A sound that can't be played (no such asset, a missing or broken file) is logged once and gives `SoundHandle.None`.
- While the listener stands in an indoor volume (Rendering's `IndoorVolume`), the `Ambience` bus is lowered and low-passed (`IndoorAmbienceGain` 0.6, `IndoorAmbienceCutoff` 1200 Hz), so the street is dull inside; Steam Audio's occlusion replaces this in step 3. While the game is paused, the `Ambience`, `Effects` and `Dialogue` buses are held, and `Music` and `Interface` play on.
- The volumes are console variables (`AudioSettings`): `audio.Master`, `audio.Music`, `audio.Ambience`, `audio.Effects`, `audio.Dialogue` and `audio.Interface`, from 0 to 1, which the settings file sets.
- Saves keep nothing of sounds: what plays is rebuilt from the scene's emitters. Of the music they keep each bus's cues and the bars they had played, and the tracks scripts asked for.

## Music and ambience

- Music is layers on a grid (owner, 2026-11-27: a bass loop plays, a drum loop joins in an area after a fill-in and leaves with a fill-out, all in tempo; the same for ambience). A `music` data asset (`Music`: `MusicCue`) has a `tempo` in beats a minute, `beatsPerBar` and `phraseBars`, which make its grid of beats, bars and phrases, a `bus` (`Music`, or `Ambience`), a `gain`, and `tracks`. A tempo of 0 makes a cue without a grid, an ambience: its tracks change at once and fade.
- A track (`MusicTrack`) has a `name`, a `loop` file and optionally a `fillIn` and a `fillOut` file, a `gain`, `fadeIn` and `fadeOut` seconds, and says when it sounds: always; with `byRequest`, only while a music area or a script asks for it by name; and only while its `when` conditions hold, checked for the listener (a fact such as `Time.Hour`, a quest's state). It comes in on the next line of the grid its `start` names (`Beat`, `Bar`, `Phrase`, or `None` for at once): the fill-in plays from that line for its length in whole bars, then the loop starts. A request that comes late in a bar therefore waits for the next bar; nothing is ever cut into a bar. It goes out on its `stop` line: the loop ends there and the fill-out plays from it. A track no longer wanted during its fill-in never starts its loop. A loop's length is its file's, rounded to whole bars (or `bars`), and with `phase` `Locked` (the default) it begins where it would be had it played since the cue began, so all loops stay in step however they come and go; `FromStart` begins at the loop's start. Loops are exported as whole bars at the cue's tempo (the owner's from Reason).
- A track with a `scatter` sound in place of a loop throws that `sound` data asset around the listener while it is wanted, every `everyMin` to `everyMax` seconds, at a random side between `nearDistance` and `farDistance` metres: far dogs, traffic. Scatter waits while the game is paused.
- `MusicSystem` (a frame system and the `IMusicPlayer` service gameplay knows: `Play`, `Stop`, `Push`, `Pop`, `RequestLayer`, `ReleaseLayer`, `PlayStinger`, `GetCurrent`, `GetPosition`, `IsLayerOn`) keeps a stack of cues for each bus, the top one heard. A cue's grid is counted in mixer frames from the frame its first bar began on, and everything is scheduled `LookaheadFrames` (1024) or more ahead as voices that start and stop on exact frames, so the game's frame rate never shows in the timing. Each frame the system finds the tracks wanted and brings the others in or out. A stopped loop fades over 5 ms at least, so it never clicks. `Play` puts a cue at the bottom of its bus's stack, taking the place of the one there on its next bar (playing the cue that is there changes nothing); `Stop` ends on the next bar.
- The cue stack is for a song over the music (owner, 2026-11-27: the hub's song over the level's music). `Push` plays the new cue's `fillIn` from the next bar of the cue heard, which plays on under it; when the fill's bars are over, the cue below is held, remembering the frames it had played, and the new cue starts. `Pop` ends the top cue on its next bar and plays its `fillOut`; when that is over the cue below continues from the bar it was held at, in phase, with the tracks wanted now and no fill-ins. A cue pushed takes its own tempo.
- A `musicArea` component (`MusicArea`: `layers`) on an entity with a `triggerArea` asks for those tracks while a `triggerActivator` stands in it (asked of the cameras' `TriggerAreaSystem` each frame, so a load that puts the player back in an area needs nothing more). A `sceneMusic` component (`SceneMusic`: `music`, `ambience`) plays a scene's cues when it is spawned; when it is gone and no other scene's took its place, both buses stop.
- `PlayStinger` plays a sound file once over the music from the next line of the cue heard (the next beat by default). The `playMusic` and `playStinger` effects and flow nodes do the same from data. `MusicBeatEvent` (the cue, the bar and the beat, from 1) is sent as the music heard reaches a beat, for anything that wants to move in time.
- Tests hear cues through the null device at 480 beats a minute over loops that hold one value a bar, so a sample tells which layers sounded on a frame (`MusicSystemTests`).
- Not yet: a pitch-keeping time stretch for loops at another tempo, streaming, gain curves over the day, a pushed cue following the tempo of the one below, continue points other than where the cue stopped, and the editor.
